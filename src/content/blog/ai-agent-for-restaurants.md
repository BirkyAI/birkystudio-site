The phone rings at 7:40 on a Friday night and there is nobody in the building who can pick it up.

The host is three steps away seating a party of nine. The pass has four tickets up and one of them is the fish. The person who could answer the phone is carrying plates, because that is what everyone is doing at 7:40 on a Friday. The call drops into the voicemail box nobody has checked since Tuesday.

The caller wanted a table for six on Saturday. She tried two more numbers, reached an actual human on the third try, and booked there. She was not annoyed with you. She never got far enough to be annoyed. She simply wanted a table, and the phone was the only door she could find.

A restaurant almost never loses a booking because the food is wrong. It loses the booking in the minute the phone rang and nobody could reach it, and that minute tends to land at exactly the hour people decide where they are eating.

## The restaurant day, hour by hour

Nobody in a restaurant is lazy, and nobody forgot to answer. Look at the day as it actually runs and the problem stops looking like a discipline problem. It starts looking like arithmetic.

| The hour | Who is free to answer | What actually happens | What it costs |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 11:15am, before the doors open | you, if the order and the prep are already done | the phone rings into an empty room and the caller does not bother leaving a message | a booking nobody ever hears about |
| 12:40pm, lunch service | nobody who is not already holding something | the call about Saturday reaches a mailbox | the table is booked somewhere that picked up |
| 3:30pm, the quiet hour | whoever is prepping for dinner | the allergy question gets "let me find out and call you back" | the call back that never happens, and a guest who does not ask twice |
| 7:40pm, dinner service | nobody at all | the call, the WhatsApp and the Instagram message arrive in the same ten minutes | the hour when the most enquiries come in, and the hour when none of them get answered |
| 9:50pm, last orders | the bar, if the bar is quiet | the private event enquiry sits unread | a Monday conversation that never starts |
| 11:20pm, at home | you, and you are not opening the laptop | several notifications, half of them from yesterday | the enquiry you will get to when you remember it |

Read the third column down the page. Not one of those rows is a staffing failure you can fix with a rota, because the same person cannot stand at the phone and at the pass. The calls do not arrive when you are free. They arrive when the restaurant is full, which is the whole point of the restaurant.

## The line that could have been written by a restaurant

An agent on a real estate technology forum put it this way last month:

> *"First responder wins, but agents can't be at their desk 24/7"*

Swap the desk for the pass and the sentence belongs to every restaurant I have ever watched. The person who answers is usually the one who gets the table. The guest is not comparing your menu to the other menu in that moment, she is comparing whoever answered. The four questions she had, whether you take walk-ins, whether the kitchen is still open, whether you can seat ten, whether there is parking nearby, do not need a chef. They need somebody to answer them.

We have written about how this plays out across every channel a small business owns, in [Missed Leads By Channel](https://birkystudio.com/blog/missed-leads-by-channel/), and a restaurant is the clearest version of it: the door is a phone, a WhatsApp thread and a DM, and all three sit in the same noisy room.

## The note you were never going to write

There is a second leak, and it happens after the good outcome.

Somebody messages at 9:20pm about a table for Saturday and mentions that one of the six is severely allergic to shellfish. Somebody else asks for the corner table because it is their mother's birthday. A third asks whether the kitchen can do something plain for a child. All three conversations happen, all three are answered, and none of them are written down anywhere, because writing them down is an extra job on top of the job.

Then Saturday arrives, the note is gone, and the guest spends the evening quietly managing the thing she already told you about when she booked. That is the part that shows up later in a review, in the shape of a sentence like "the staff did not seem to know".

## What the agent does in a restaurant

- Answers the phone, WhatsApp, the Instagram DM and the website enquiry form, day and night, at 12:40pm and at 11:20pm, in the same tone
- Handles the repeated questions directly: opening hours, walk-ins, tables for a group, the set menu, what is on the kitchen's late menu, parking, allergies
- Books the table, holds it to your rules, sends the confirmation, and reminds the guest the day before so the no-show does not happen
- Writes the note: the allergy, the birthday, the request for the corner table, the reason they are coming, attached to the guest rather than to a scrap of paper at the host stand
- Keeps one running record per guest, so the second visit begins where the first one ended
- Follows up with the guest who asked about a private event and then went quiet, and with the enquiry that arrived while you were closed for two days
- Speaks English and Spanish, and it stays in the voice you give it, so it does not sound like a call centre reading a script
- Flags anything it should not handle alone, a complaint, a large function, a supplier call, straight to a person with the details attached

- It will not invent a table you do not have. It books against the rules you set: how many covers you release, how long a hold lasts, which nights you would rather keep for walk-ins
