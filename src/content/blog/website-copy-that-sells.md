You've got a beautiful website. The colors are perfect, the layout is clean, and it looks great on mobile. But the phone isn't ringing. The contact form is empty. Visitors land on your site and leave without doing anything.

The problem probably isn't your design. It's your words.

Good website copy is the difference between a visitor who bounces in five seconds and one who becomes a paying customer. And the good news? You don't need to be a professional writer to get it right. You just need to follow a few simple principles.

## Write for Your Customer, Not for Yourself

The biggest mistake small business owners make is writing about themselves instead of their customers. Your website shouldn't start with "We are a company that..." or "Founded in 2015, we pride ourselves on..."

Instead, start with your customer's problem.

Here's the shift:

**Before:** "We offer professional web design services with 10 years of experience."

**After:** "Your customers are searching for you online. If your website doesn't show up or look professional, they'll hire someone else."

See the difference? The first version talks about you. The second version talks about what your customer cares about. Always lead with their problem, then show how you solve it.

## Use the Words Your Customers Use

Skip the jargon. If you're an accountant, don't write "We provide comprehensive financial reconciliation services." Write "We handle your bookkeeping so you can focus on running your business."

Your customers don't search for industry terminology. They search for solutions to their problems. Use the same language they would use when describing their situation to a friend.

One trick: look at your Google reviews, your WhatsApp messages, or your customer emails. How do real customers describe what they needed? Those exact phrases are gold for your website copy.

## Every Page Needs One Clear Goal

Each page on your website should have one job. One action you want the visitor to take.

- Your homepage? Get them to contact you or view your services.
- A service page? Get them to request a quote.
- Your about page? Build trust so they reach out.

If a page tries to do five things at once, it does none of them well. Pick one call-to-action per page and make it obvious. Use a button that stands out, with text that says exactly what happens next.

"Get Your Free Quote" beats "Submit." "See Our Work" beats "Learn More." Be specific about what they'll get when they click.

## Structure Your Copy for Scanners

Here's a reality check: most people don't read your website. They scan it.

They look at the headline, maybe the subheadings, and the buttons. If something catches their eye, they slow down and read more. If not, they leave.

To work with this behavior:

- **Use clear headings** that tell a story on their own. Someone reading only your H2s should understand your offer.
- **Keep paragraphs short.** Two to three sentences max. Big blocks of text scare people away.
- **Use bullet points** for lists of benefits or features.
- **Bold key phrases** so scanners can grab the important bits.
- **Add white space.** Give your text room to breathe. Cramped text feels overwhelming.

## Lead with Benefits, Not Features

A feature is what your product or service does. A benefit is what your customer gets from it.

- Feature: "24/7 AI chatbot for your website."
- Benefit: "Your customers get instant answers at any hour, even when you're sleeping."

- Feature: "Mobile-responsive design."
- Benefit: "Your site looks perfect on every phone, so you never lose a customer on the go."

Always ask yourself: "So what?" after every feature you list. That answer is your benefit, and that's what goes on the website.

## Add Social Proof Where It Matters

People trust other people more than they trust your marketing copy. Sprinkle proof throughout your site, not just on a testimonials page.

- Drop a customer quote right below your main headline.
- Add a Google review rating near your contact form.
- Mention the number of clients you've served on your services page.
- Include a short case study or before-and-after example.

Real proof from real people removes doubt. And less doubt means more clicks on that contact button.

## Your About Page Is Actually Important

A lot of business owners treat the about page as an afterthought. But it's usually one of the most visited pages on any website.

People want to know who they're doing business with. They want to see your face, understand your story, and decide if they trust you.

Keep it personal. Share why you started your business. Mention what drives you. Add a real photo of yourself or your team. This isn't the place for a corporate mission statement. It's the place to show you're a real person who cares about their work.

## Make It Easy to Take the Next Step

You'd be surprised how many websites make it hard to get in touch. The contact information is buried in the footer, or the form has ten required fields, or there's no phone number visible.

Put your contact method front and center. In Guatemala, that usually means WhatsApp. Add a WhatsApp button that's visible on every page. Keep your forms short: name, phone or email, and a brief message. That's all you need to start a conversation.

If someone has to hunt for a way to reach you, they won't bother.
