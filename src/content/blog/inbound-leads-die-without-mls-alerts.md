Let me tell you about a lead named Anna.

Anna lands on your real estate site at 3:42 on a Tuesday. She fills in the buyer inquiry: three bedrooms, under $450k, Lakeview neighborhood. It's a good lead. She's pre-approved, she's serious, and she's the exact buyer you spent money acquiring.

Now watch what actually happens to Anna.

Her inquiry hits your inbox. From there, everything depends on one fragile, manual step: somebody has to translate that inquiry into an MLS alert and hit send. Not a system. Not an automation. A person, in the middle of everything else, remembering to do it.

And here's the ugly truth I hear from agents over and over — in their own words:

> *"I have lead inquiries that come in all day. I rely on our agents to hook them up for MLS alerts via our IDX — but barely anyone does it."*

One agent on Reddit said it exactly like that. **"Barely anyone does it."** Not "sometimes we miss one." *Barely anyone.*

So Anna waits. An agent "means to" set up her search, but they're showing houses, they're at a closing, they're on the phone with a lender. The lead sits. Maybe she gets one hurried call, maybe she gets none. Meanwhile her phone still works — she's checking Zillow, she's contacting another agent, she's answering the first email that actually sends her a match.

Anna was never a bad lead. She was just never hooked up.

## The bottleneck isn't lead capture. It's the search setup.

The painful truth about the leads that pour into your website: **they don't die because people don't fill out the form. They die at the step right after.**

Capturing the inquiry is the easy 10%. The hard 90% is the chain of manual work between "inquiry arrives" and "lead is engaged." Somewhere in that chain sits the MLS alert — and if it never gets built, every inbound dollar you spent goes to a name that fades away by Friday.

Most agents don't lose leads because of one big mistake. They lose them because the quiet, boring, forgettable setup work simply never happens. "Barely anyone does it" isn't laziness — it's that manual setup is the first thing to slip when you're out in the field doing the actual job.

## What a dead lead actually costs you

Let's put a number on Anna, because "leads die" sounds abstract until you price it.

Say you're spending on your website, your Google Business Profile, and your social ads to keep the inbound pipeline full — that's the cost of *getting* Anna to fill out a form. Every inquiry that ever reaches your inbox already carries that acquisition cost. When it goes silent, you don't just lose the commission — you lose the money you already paid to bring her to you, plus the financial hit of a warm buyer that a faster agent is now working.

The first responder wins. I've written before about [why a five-minute response changes everything](/blog/lead-response-speed-5min/), and it applies double here — because Anna isn't just expecting a fast reply, she's expecting you to know what she wants and show her options. The agent who both answers fast *and* has her search live is the agent she works with.

That's the whole game: **respond instantly, and send her a stream of houses that match.** Do those two things and she's engaged. Fail at either and she's gone.
