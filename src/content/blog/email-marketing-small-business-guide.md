You have probably heard that email marketing is dead. It is not. For every $1 spent on email marketing, the average return is $36. That makes it the highest-ROI digital marketing channel available to small businesses, beating social media, paid ads, and SEO.

The best part? You do not need a big budget, a design team, or technical skills to get started. You just need a plan and a free (or cheap) email tool.

## Why Email Marketing Still Works

Social media algorithms change constantly. Your posts might reach 5% of your followers on a good day. Paid ads stop working the moment you stop paying. But an email list? That is yours. You own those contacts.

Here is why email works so well for small businesses:

- **Direct access.** Your message lands in someone's inbox, not buried in a feed.
- **High intent.** People who give you their email address already trust you.
- **Measurable.** You can see exactly who opened, clicked, and bought.
- **Affordable.** Most email platforms are free for your first 500 to 1,000 subscribers.

## Step 1: Choose an Email Platform

You do not need to overthink this. Here are three solid options for small businesses:

- **Mailchimp** — The most popular choice. Free up to 500 contacts. Easy drag-and-drop editor.
- **Brevo (formerly Sendinblue)** — Free up to 300 emails per day. Good for businesses that send more frequently.
- **MailerLite** — Free up to 1,000 subscribers. Clean interface, great for beginners.

Pick one, create an account, and move on. You can always switch later.

## Step 2: Build Your Email List

This is where most small businesses get stuck. They think they need thousands of subscribers before they can start. Wrong. You can start with 10 people.

Here is how to collect email addresses the right way:

**Add a signup form to your website.** Place it in your footer, on your homepage, and on a dedicated landing page. Keep it simple: name and email address. Nothing more.

**Offer something valuable.** People need a reason to hand over their email. Some ideas:

- A discount code (10% off first purchase)
- A free guide or checklist related to your business
- Early access to sales or new products
- A free consultation or quote

**Ask in person.** If you have a physical location, ask customers at checkout if they want to join your email list for updates and special offers. A tablet or a simple paper signup sheet works fine.

**Add a link to your social media bios.** Point your Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp Business profiles to your signup page.

**Important:** Never buy email lists. It hurts your deliverability, annoys people, and can get your account banned. Build your list organically.

## Step 3: Send Emails People Actually Want to Read

The biggest mistake small businesses make with email is sending boring, generic newsletters that nobody asked for. Here is how to avoid that:

**Write like a human.** Skip the corporate tone. Write the way you would talk to a customer in person. Short sentences. Simple words. Get to the point.

**Provide value.** Every email should do one of these things:

- Teach something useful
- Share a helpful tip or resource
- Announce a sale or special offer
- Tell a story about your business or customers

**Keep it short.** Most people read emails on their phones. Aim for 200 to 400 words. If you have more to say, link to a blog post on your website.

**Use a clear subject line.** This is the single most important part of your email. Some formulas that work:

- "How to [solve a problem your customers have]"
- "[Number] tips for [something relevant]"
- "You are invited: [event or offer]"
- "Quick question about [topic]"

Avoid clickbait, ALL CAPS, and too many exclamation marks. Those land you in spam.

## Step 4: Set a Realistic Schedule

You do not need to email every day. For most small businesses, once a week or twice a month is plenty. The key is consistency.

Pick a schedule you can actually stick to. If that means one email every two weeks, that is fine. Your subscribers will appreciate quality over quantity.

Some businesses do well with automated emails that go out based on customer actions:

- **Welcome email** — Sent automatically when someone joins your list
- **Abandoned cart email** — Reminds customers about items they left in their online cart
- **Birthday or anniversary email** — A simple message with a small discount
- **Follow-up email** — Sent a few days after a purchase asking for a review

Most email platforms let you set up these automations for free. They run in the background while you focus on your business.

## Step 5: Track What Works

After a few emails, check your numbers. The two most important metrics are:

- **Open rate** — The percentage of people who opened your email. Aim for 20% or higher.
- **Click rate** — The percentage who clicked a link inside. Aim for 2% or higher.

If your open rates are low, try different subject lines. If your click rates are low, make your call-to-action clearer and more specific.

Do not stress about the numbers in the beginning. What matters is that you are building a direct line to your customers, one email at a time.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Sending without permission.** Always get explicit consent before adding someone to your list.
- **Only selling.** If every email is a sales pitch, people will unsubscribe. Follow the 80/20 rule: 80% value, 20% promotion.
- **Ignoring mobile users.** Over 60% of emails are opened on phones. Use a single-column layout and large fonts.
- **Not having an unsubscribe link.** It is legally required in most countries, and it builds trust.
