## The feeds are not the problem

On r/RealEstateTechnology an agent asked for something very specific:

> "Pull land development notices and updates from city, county, and state agencies... and put together a 'Guess Who's Coming Soon' type of newsletter."

Read the replies and nearly all of the advice is plumbing. Which agency portals to pull from. How to get the agenda PDFs into one place. Where to keep what you find. Nobody argues with the plumbing, and I am not going to either.

But plumbing is not why commercial work gets missed.

Every one of those notices is already public. Free. Published by a department whose entire job is publishing them. An application to turn a corner lot into a mixed-use building lands on a planning agenda months before a single tree comes down. Access was never the problem.

The problem is that a feed is not a newsletter. A feed hands you everything and decides nothing. A newsletter hands you four lines a month and a reason to pick up the phone. That distinction is the whole post, and it is easier to show than to describe.

## Fourteen months, five issues

*The project below is a composite, built from the public records behind commercial projects I have watched go up in my own market. The months are illustrative. The shape is not.*

One parcel. A family-held corner lot, owned since the late 1980s by people who had no development plan for it. Below is what one monthly digest would have said, starting fourteen months before the hoarding went up.

```
Issue 1  (Month 0)
Filed      rezoning application, corner lot, parcel 017-443-002
Applicant  an LLC registered eleven months ago, no website
Where      40 words, page 14 of the planning commission agenda
Window     the owner is a family trust, not a developer. Nothing is promised to anyone yet.
```

```
Issue 2  (Month 3)
Filed      pre-application meeting notes enter the public file, traffic study commissioned
Applicant  now has a civil engineer and a sketch of the site plan
Where      the same file, second tab, plus the minutes of one committee meeting
Window     two commercial tenants in that trade area are being approached by a broker you have never met
```

```
Issue 3  (Month 7)
Filed      rezoning approved with conditions, site plan review scheduled
Applicant  the LLC now owns an option on the lot, not the lot itself
Where      the hearing minute, four lines, one condition attached
Window     the two houses either side of the lot are still single-family. One of them lists next spring.
```

```
Issue 4  (Month 11)
Filed      demolition permit application, building permit application
Applicant  a builder, a lender and a general contractor are all now visible in the record
Where      the permit portal, updated quietly on a Tuesday
Window     the last month in which a letter, a call or a broker opinion is early rather than late
```

```
Issue 5  (Month 14)
Filed      hoarding up, trees down, equipment on site
Applicant  the same people, now returning your competitor's calls
Where      you drove past it
Window     none. This is the issue everybody reads.
```

Read the Window line under each issue again. Only the first two issues are cheap. Issue 1 cost an hour and a phone call to a family trust that has owned a lot for thirty-eight years and has fielded one offer for it. Issue 5 costs nothing, arrives on its own, and by then forty people know the same thing you do.

The middle earn their place too. Issue 3 is the one nobody thinks to read, because by then the interesting part feels decided. The conditional approval is where the shape of the building gets set, and where the neighboring parcels stop being someone's garden and start being someone's assembly.

## Why the first issue never gets read

Three reasons, and none of them is laziness.

**It does not look like an opportunity.** A page number, a parcel number and a name you do not recognize is not a deal, it is a paragraph. There are no renderings in month 0 and there never are. The version of this story that feels like news is fourteen months away, and it is free.

**Nobody owns the reading.** Monitoring a planning portal is not billable, has no deadline and belongs to no role in a small brokerage. It is the same shape as every other step that quietly does not happen, and I have written about that pattern before ([Inbound Leads Die Without MLS Alerts](https://birkystudio.com/blog/inbound-leads-die-without-mls-alerts/)).

**The document keeps changing its name.** The same project appears as an application, then a notice, then a hearing minute, then a permit, and each one lives on a different system on a different clock. I went through exactly this with auction lists ([Five Auction Mornings, One Wrong List](https://birkystudio.com/blog/five-auction-mornings-one-wrong-list/)), and the lesson transfers: a published document is not the authority, it is one witness.

## What it will not do

- **It will not tell you the project gets approved.** A large share of rezoning applications change shape or die, and an early signal is a window, not a promise.
- **It cannot read a notice that was never published.** Some jurisdictions post late, some post only in a scanned minute, and a few post nothing at all until the permit.
- **It will not call a paragraph an opportunity.** You make that call. We hand you the four lines and the date; the judgment stays where the license is.
- **It will not fix the reasons above.** It can find the signal fourteen months early. It cannot make anybody own the reading, and it cannot make a thin month feel urgent, which is exactly when acting is worth money.
- **It refuses to quote a conversion rate.** Nobody has ever measured how many commercial deals come out of public records in your market, and anyone who hands you a number made it up.
