There is a comment from r/RealEstateTechnology that has stayed with me, because it is not really a complaint about technology. It is a complaint about reading the mail:

> "Pull land development notices and updates from city, county, and state agencies... and put together a 'Guess Who's Coming Soon' type of newsletter."

The person asking is not asking for a nicer dashboard. They are asking for someone to sit there and read. Because everything they want to know is already public. A proposed gas station, a new grocery anchor, a rezoned corner lot, a big-box pad site: none of it is a secret. It is filed, dated, and published, weeks or months before a single truck shows up.

The information is not hidden. It is just unread.

## The opportunity window nobody is watching

Commercial development is one of the few markets where the signal arrives before the money moves. You do not have to guess what is coming. You have to be the kind of business that actually looks.

Here is what the public process looks like, stage by stage, and what each stage is worth to whoever reads it first:

| Stage | What is already on the public record | What it is worth to whoever sees it first |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1. Application filed | Developer name, parcel, requested use, filing date | The first conversation. Land, or a relationship, before anyone else knows a project exists |
| 2. Agenda published | Hearing date, project scope, sometimes the tenants attached | Tenant rep and early intelligence, while the plan can still bend |
| 3. Public hearing | Who objected, traffic findings, conditions of approval | The difference between what will get built and what quietly dies |
| 4. Permit issued | Approved use, square footage, rough timeline | Pre-construction positioning, leasing, service contracts, staffing |
| 5. Ground breaks | A sign, then a fence, then a building | Nothing. Everyone knows now. The window has closed |

Read the middle column again. That is a lead. The right column is a clock, and it is always running.

By the time commercial activity is obvious enough to notice on a drive, the only people still able to act are the ones who read the record back at stage one.

## Why nobody does it (and why it never gets fixed)

This is the same shape as most of the problems business owners bring us, and it is the one we come back to again and again: the data exists, and pulling it takes one step per feed, forever.

Development notices are not in one place. They live across dozens of portals. City planning departments, county commissions, state agencies, and planning & zoning boards each publish on their own schedule, in their own format, in their own corner of the web. There is no single "here is what is coming" page.

So the job falls to a busy human, and a busy human has a business to run. Checking twenty feeds on a Monday is a nice idea that lasts about three weeks. This is the same reason comps never get pulled as often as they should, and the same reason inbound leads quietly never get their MLS alerts set up: the step that would create the value is one step, repeated by hand, on top of everything else. So it silently does not happen.

You can read more about that pattern in our post on [why inbound leads die without MLS alerts](/blog/inbound-leads-die-without-mls-alerts/), because it is the exact same problem wearing different clothes.

## This is a monitoring job, and monitoring is what an AI agent is for

Here is the part that makes this a clean fit. You do not need a human to decide what to do with a rezoning notice. You need someone to hand you the notice.

That is a monitoring job, not a judgment job, and monitoring is the one thing an AI agent does better than any of us. A Birky Studio agent set up for this will:

- Watch the city, county, and state feeds you actually care about, on a schedule, without being asked
- Surface a running "who is coming soon" digest: project scope, the developer, any tenants attached, and where it sits in the P&Z process
- Flag what changed since the last time it looked, so you are reading updates, not re-reading everything
- Keep a record, so when a project takes eight months to move you still have the full history instead of a vague memory

It works the same way we pull market data on demand for property questions, which we wrote about in [market data and comps without the tool churn](/blog/market-data-comps-on-demand/). One place asks the question, the agent goes and reads, and you get an answer instead of another tab.

## What it will not do

I am not going to sell you a crystal ball. A filed notice is not a finished project. Plans get withdrawn, financing falls through, and hearings get continued, often. The agent reports what the record says and tells you when it changes. It does not pretend a stage one filing is a done deal, and neither should you.

What it removes is not the uncertainty. It removes the reason you never saw the opportunity in the first place.
