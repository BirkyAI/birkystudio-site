# Missed Leads by Channel: Where They Leak

A buyer sends an Instagram DM at 9:14 pm asking if a listing is still available. The next morning a form comes in through the website, and an hour later someone texts the number on your sign. By the time you sit down with coffee, two of those three have cooled off and you honestly cannot remember which one you answered.

Here is the uncomfortable part. You do not have a lead problem. You have a channel problem. Every place a lead can reach you is a separate inbox with its own rules, and each one leaks in a slightly different way.

## Where your leads actually arrive

Most small businesses and agents now field inbound messages from six places at once:

- A phone call, often while you are already with a client
- A text message to your mobile
- A WhatsApp message
- An email to the address on your website
- A direct message on Instagram or Facebook
- A form submitted on your site

Each of these feels manageable on its own. Together they are a full-time job nobody assigned you. And unlike a real job, no one notices when the shift goes uncovered.

## The channel-by-channel leak

| Channel | Where it usually dies | What it actually needs |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Phone call | You are driving, in a showing, or asleep. It rolls to voicemail and the caller does not try again. | An answer every time, or a text back within a minute. |
| Text message | It lands while you are mid-task and scrolls out of view. | A reply within minutes, plus a reason to stay in touch. |
| WhatsApp | Read at 11 pm, answered never. | A same-day response and one thread that stays in one place. |
| Email | Sorted into "later," which is another word for never. | A fast first reply and a follow-up that runs without you. |
| Instagram or Facebook DM | Seen in the notifications pile, buried by everything above it. | Someone watching the messages, not just the feed. |
| Website form | It reaches you as a notification you open on the fly and then forget. | An instant reply and a logged, followable record. |

Read the middle column again. None of those rows describes a hard problem. They all describe the same moment: a message arrives, and the one person who could answer it is busy, asleep, or scrolling past.

## The step nobody owns

This is the pattern worth naming. When a lead comes in on any of those channels, exactly one step decides whether it becomes a deal: somebody writes back, then logs it somewhere they will actually find again.

On a team, everyone assumes someone else does it. On your own, you assume you will remember. Both are the same bet, and it is the bet that quietly loses the lead.

If you have ever said "I will reply to that after this appointment," you already know how the story ends. The appointment runs long, the next one starts, and the message is forty notifications deep by dinner. Nobody decided to ignore it. The step just had no owner.

It is the same shape as the MLS-alert gap so many agents describe in [another post of ours](/blog/inbound-leads-die-without-mls-alerts/): the interest arrives, and the one connecting step never gets done.

One missed lead a week, at a few thousand dollars of value each, adds up fast. If two of those a month would have converted, the yearly number is bigger than any tool you are comparing. The leak is not dramatic. It is just constant.
