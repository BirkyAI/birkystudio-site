Ask a customer how they found their last plumber and you will get a shrug and a sentence like "I asked ChatGPT."

That is the new front door. A customer types one plain question, the assistant answers with three businesses and a line about each, and the customer calls one of them. The industry calls these AI answer engines. The honest version is this: something decided which three names to say out loud, and it was not you.

The reason is not that you are worse or more expensive than the three. The reason is whether the assistant could read anything trustworthy about your business at all.

Agents describe the same shape of problem on their own side of it:

> *"Lack of good data sources for property analysis"*

An assistant pointed at your business inherits exactly that problem. It can only repeat what it can find, and what it can find is whatever is easy to read, consistent everywhere it appears, and written in the plain language of the question. If your prices are nowhere, your services are a pile of adjectives, and your business name is spelled two different ways across four profiles, there is nothing solid enough for it to say out loud.

So this is not a visibility problem in the usual marketing sense. It is a readability problem, in the machine sense.

## The work that fixes it is unglamorous

Nobody puts this on a keynote slide and nobody demos it, because the fixes are small, boring and structural. The industry said it well first:

> *"The tools agents and TCs actually stick with are the boring ones... the real progress is usually a small thing that removes one annoying step and just works."*

Being named by an assistant is that idea, applied to being found. The assistant does not care about your new logo or the carousel on your homepage. It cares about this:

- One page per thing you actually sell, written as the answer to the question a customer asks
- Prices in plain numbers on the page, instead of "contact us for a quote"
- One identical business name, address and phone number everywhere you appear
- Specific sentences, not "solutions for every need"
- Pages that load fast enough for a person and a machine
- New content that keeps answering questions month after month

*What follows is our own work, not a client's. We audited both of our own websites for this rather than dressing up somebody else's numbers.*

## What we found on our own sites

We published an audit of these two sites months ago and it was not flattering: 481 pages, 443 titles truncated in Google, 16 orphan pages, 11 dead share images. [That audit is still up](https://birkystudio.com/blog/seo-audit-481-pages/).

Going back through both sites for answer engines, three problems came up, and none of them were about design.

1. **The headings answered questions nobody asks.** "Our Process" is not an answer. "How much does a website cost?" is.
2. **The plain numbers were missing.** The pages said every business is different. True, and useless to something that has to finish a sentence.
3. **The same facts disagreed between pages.** Two price lists from different years, and one page still promising a service we had retired. Read two versions of a price and you tend to say neither.

So we fixed the boring parts. One page per service, each opening with the customer's own question. Price and delivery time stated on the page instead of buried behind a form. The same details everywhere we appear. And every one of those pages written so an answer engine can lift one clean sentence instead of guessing at three.
