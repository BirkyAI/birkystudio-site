There is a thread in r/RealEstateTechnology where an agent is weighing up a portal lead program, and the whole question is about numbers. How many leads per week. What they cost per lead. Whether the quality holds up. Fair questions, and the replies argue about them for a hundred comments.

Then, at the very end of the original post, one sentence that almost nobody answers:

> "My concern from what I've read is that they try and micromanage your team to close, and also push Zillow Home Loans."

That is the clause. Not the price, not the volume. Every agent I have ever talked to about a lead program spent the whole meeting on the two questions that appear on the invoice, and signed without reading the third one.

## A lead program sells you two things

The first is names. That is what it looks like you are buying, and it is what the sales call is about.

The second is a method. A program cannot grade you on how fast you respond unless it decides what "responding" means, so it decides. It cannot report on follow-up unless it defines what follow-up is, so it defines it. You are not just renting access to strangers. You are adopting somebody else's way of working a lead, and that method comes with a manual that is not written for you.

Here is that method, printed the way a program will describe it to you.

```
Minute 0 to 5    call, then a text, then an email
Day 1            second call, a different hour than the first
Day 2            one listing that matches what they asked for
Day 4            third call
Day 7            a market update in the same thread
Day 14           a check-in with no ask attached to it
Day 21           last attempt, then park the record
                 every attempt logged the same day, by you
```

Read that twice. There is nothing wrong in it. There is nothing in it I would take out, and nothing in it I would add. If you ran those eight lines on the 400 names already sitting in your phone, your pipeline would not look the way it looks right now.

That is the genuinely uncomfortable part of a lead program: it is not selling a shortcut, it is selling discipline. And discipline works. Which is exactly why the rest of the arrangement matters more than the cost per lead.

## The three things the method leaves out

**One: who runs the minutes in between.** The cadence assumes somebody answers. At seven in the morning and at nine at night and on a Sunday. If that somebody is you, between appointments, then it is not a cadence at all, it is a to-do list, and every unanswered attempt gets recorded as a miss on a scoreboard that belongs to the company selling you the names.

**Two: it only runs on names from their pipe.** Your sphere, your referrals, the people who walked into your open house, the buyer who texted you in March about schools: none of that is inside the program, so nothing runs on it. Not out of spite. Simply because those names are not the product.

**Three: the record it produces is not yours.** Every attempt, every note, every conversation sits in their database, because that is the database being graded. Which is the part that quietly decides whether you can ever leave.

## The re-typing is the lock-in

When one of those leads arrives, it lands in their inbox with their notes attached. For it to be useful to you it has to get into your calendar, your phone and your CRM, which means you type it again. And next week, when the next one arrives, you type that one too.

You have become the integration between two systems that were never asked to talk to each other. It is the same unpaid job I wrote about in [The Unpaid Job Eating Your Commission](/blog/integration-hell-real-estate-ai-agent/), except this time the data is a stranger you paid for rather than a client you already had.

That is what makes leaving feel expensive. It is not the monthly fee, it is the two years of attempt history and three hundred records you would be walking away from, because the record of your work lives on somebody else's shelf. I made the money argument about portal programs in [Portal Leads Are a Lease, Not a Purchase](/blog/portal-leads-are-a-lease-not-a-purchase/) and I will not repeat it here. This post is about the other clause: who owns the way you work.
