## The question is not about a login

A seller posted this to r/RealEstateTechnology on 11 November:

> "I had initially seen that I should get an email from BrokerBay to set up my sellers portal, where I could see showing agents and their feedback. I haven't gotten that. My agent put my email address in... My agent is older and said he's honestly not sure what's available to see on the seller's side."

Read it again and notice what she is actually asking. She is not asking for a login. She is asking whether anybody liked her house.

She knows how many showings there were. She can confirm and decline appointment requests. What she cannot see is the part she cares about: who walked through, what they said on the way out, and how warm any of it was. Her own listing agent does not know what the seller side of the platform exposes, so the tool she is nominally paying for is a feature nobody has ever shown her.

That is the version of this problem people argue about, and it is the smaller half. Whether the portal has a feedback tab is a product question. Whether your seller hears anything at all is your problem.

## The answer exists. It is sitting in your voicemail.

Here is the uncomfortable part. The information the seller wants was captured. It was captured well, by a professional, on the day it happened. It simply went somewhere she will never look.

*The transcript below is a composite, built from the feedback calls I have listened to over the years. The details move around from deal to deal. The shape of it does not.*

```
VOICEMAIL   Tue 6:42pm   0:41

"Hi, it's Dana from Coastal Realty, we just
came out of your listing on Palmer.

Quick one for you. Buyers liked it more than
they expected, they had written it off from
the photos. Two things though. She thinks the
kitchen is smaller than the plan suggested,

They are stretching to get here, so price is

Call me back and I'll walk you through it."
```

Six facts, in forty-one seconds: the buyers' reaction to the photos against the house itself, the kitchen objection, the second question about the roof and the furnace, the financing ceiling, the requested second showing, and a competing listing. Any listing agent would call that a good showing.

Now here is what the seller knew by Friday.

```
Seller update, week of November 3

"Two showings this week, both went well.
Will keep you posted."
```

Eleven words against six facts. Not because anybody lied or hid anything. Because a voicemail is a place, and the seller is not in it.

## Three reasons it stops there

**It arrives at the wrong minute.** Feedback lands in the ten minutes after a showing ends, which is the ten minutes you are driving, or locking up, or already late for the next appointment. Listening to it happens later, in the car, and that is where it stays.

**The platform logs the appointment, not the conversation.** Booking systems are built to confirm a time slot. A confirm button is not a comment box, and the sentence a buyer agent says out loud has no field waiting for it on either side of the transaction.

**Telling the seller is nobody's job.** The showing belonged to the buyer's agent. The listing belongs to you. The update falls into the gap between two people who each assume the other one handled it, and it is never billable, which is exactly why it is the first thing dropped in a busy week.

## Your seller is spending trust on your silence

This is the part that costs money. A seller who hears nothing does not conclude that nothing is happening. They conclude that they are being handled.

So they start checking the listing views themselves. They ask a friend who is in the business. They form opinions about whether you are working the listing, and they carry those opinions into the next conversation, which is how a fifteen minute check-in becomes a ninety minute appointment. Agents talk about this one constantly: appointments running long because they cannot instantly account for the last two weeks ([Clients Remember What You Forget](https://birkystudio.com/blog/clients-remember-what-you-forget/) is the same gap from the other side of the table).

I have written about the later stage of it too, the client who messages every few days because nothing in the process gives them visibility, and the agent who becomes the tracker of record for dates that were already written down ([The Escrow Dates Trapped in the PDF](https://birkystudio.com/blog/escrow-dates-trapped-in-the-pdf/)). Same disease, earlier in the deal. The fix is duller than it sounds: somebody has to be the one who writes it down and sends it.

## What the agent does instead

- **Asks four questions within a minute of the showing ending.** How did it go, what did they react to, what did they question, and is there a second visit in them. Sent to the buyer's agent by message, while the visit is still fresh enough to remember.
- **Writes the answer into one record for the listing.** Not a folder of screenshots, not your memory of a call. Every showing and every piece of feedback in one running note, so the pattern across twenty showings is visible instead of twenty separate anecdotes.
- **Sends the seller a short weekly note.** Who came, what they said, what it means, and what changes next week. Short enough that you would not mind your own seller forwarding it to a friend.
- **Flags the one thing that needs a decision.** The kitchen objection repeated by three buyers is not feedback, it is a presentation or pricing conversation, and it should reach you in week two rather than at the ninety day review.
- **Does the chasing.** A buyer agent who does not answer the first message gets a second one. Unanswered feedback is rarely withheld on purpose, it is just never typed up by somebody who also has a job.

## What it will not do

- It will not make a buyer like the house, and it will not turn a lukewarm showing into an offer.
- It cannot collect feedback from a buyer agent who never answers. It can ask twice, and it will report silence as silence instead of dressing it up.
- It will not pass on a buyer's private finances. A pre-approval ceiling matters to your negotiation and does not belong in a note the seller forwards to her brother.
- It will not invent a conversion rate. If your listing showed nine times and produced one offer, I am not going to tell you what that number should have been.
