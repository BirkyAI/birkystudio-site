Every week somebody asks me a version of the same question, and I have learned to hear it coming. "I know I need AI in my business. I just do not know where to start."

I understand why it is so hard to answer. There are two obvious answers and both of them are wrong. The first is to keep waiting until you understand all of it, which usually means you never start at all. The second is to buy the biggest package on the list because it sounds like it covers everything, and then watch it sit half-used because you were not ready for it yet.

The honest answer is smaller than either one. Start with the single step that is costing you the most money, and buy only that.

For most small businesses, that one step is answering the phone.

## The caller is not waiting for you

Here is the part nobody explains when you are the one person doing everything in your business: the customer calling you is not comparing your quality to anyone else's. They are comparing whoever answers first.

A missed call is not a message that sits quietly and waits for you. It is a person who is already dialing the next name on their list before your call has finished going to voicemail. Plumbers, salons, clinics, shops, they all lose work this exact way. Real estate agents talk about it constantly in the forums I read: "First responder wins, but agents can't be at their desk 24/7." Swap the nouns and it is your business too. The work does not disappear because you were busy. It disappears because you were busy at the exact moment somebody was ready to pay you.

That is the step that costs the most, and it is the one worth fixing first.

## What $297 actually buys

The smallest real fix is the Text Receptionist, and it costs $297, one time.

It answers on WhatsApp, day and night. It handles the handful of questions you answer all day anyway: your hours, your prices, where you are, whether you have room this week. It takes down the person's name and number. It books them in, or it flags them for you so nothing slips.

The part people underestimate is the record. You know the note you meant to write after a phone call and never did? That is exactly the note that vanishes and then costs you a deal three weeks later, when you cannot remember what was said or what you promised. The receptionist writes that note for you, every single time, without being asked. It is not glamorous. It is just the step you were never going to keep doing by hand, done for you.

## What $297 does not buy

Honesty matters more here than a longer feature list, so here is the real boundary.

$297 does not buy you a voice agent that talks out loud on the phone. It does not build you a CRM. It does not chase your old customers for you, and it does not hand you a list of new leads. If your actual problem is one of those things, then $297 is the wrong answer, and I would rather tell you that than sell you the wrong thing and lose your trust on day one.

## Why starting small is not a compromise

People worry that starting at the bottom is a soft option, like they are buying the beginner version and will have to throw it away. It is not, and it is not thrown away.

You learn your own traffic first. You find out how many people actually call, what they actually ask, and at what hours. Almost nobody knows those numbers before they start, and almost everybody guesses them wrong. The small step pays for itself while you decide about the big one, so the decision is made with real numbers instead of a feeling.

And the upgrade is additive, not a restart. When you move up the ladder, the thing you already own keeps working. Nothing gets ripped out and rebuilt. That is the opposite of how most software switches feel, and it is deliberate.
