Every so often a set of numbers lands in a Reddit thread and does the arguing for you. This one came out of r/RealEstateTechnology, from an operator looking at the gap between what renters ask for and what they actually get:

> *"65% of residents want security deposit alternatives, but only 29% of managers actually offer them. 71% value bundled services (insurance, utilities, amenities), yet only 22% of managers provide that option."*

Read that twice. The demand is not in question. More than six in ten renters want one thing and fewer than three in ten are offered it. Seven in ten want bundled services and barely two in ten get the option. That is not a market that has not decided. That is a market where the decision has been made and the rollout never happened.

And here is the part that matters: **this is not a technology gap.** Deposit alternatives exist. Bundled service partners exist. Utility setup at move-in exists and is fully built. Every one of these is a phone call and a policy, not a build. The tools are sitting on the shelf.

So why is the middle column so empty?

## Rolling out is not a decision. It is a hundred small steps.

Here is what actually happens when a property manager decides to offer deposit alternatives. The decision takes one meeting. Then the real work starts, and the real work is not the decision, it is this, repeated for every single resident who moves in:

- mention the option at the right moment
- explain how it works, because the first ten residents will all ask the same three questions
- send the link
- chase the ones who did not click
- confirm it landed before the lease signs
- log what was offered and what was taken

Six steps. Per resident. Forever.

Multiply that by a building, then by a portfolio, and you can see exactly why the middle column of that Reddit post is what it is. Nobody sat down and decided to withhold deposit alternatives from 36% of the residents who wanted them. It is just that the six steps fall to a leasing office that is already checking people in, showing units, and handling maintenance calls, and the steps with no deadline are the first ones to quietly stop.

That is the rollout gap. It is not a technology problem wearing a technology costume, it is arithmetic.

## The order to roll them out in

Once you see the rollout as a sequence of repeating steps rather than a launch event, the order stops being a matter of taste. You roll out the option with the highest demand and the lowest per-resident cost first, prove the pattern, then stack the next one on top.

**First: utility setup at move-in.** Managers themselves name this the single biggest friction point at move-in, and it is the easiest win, because the resident is already expecting to do something on that day. There is no selling involved, just a link delivered at the exact moment they are already thinking about it.

**Second: deposit alternatives.** This is the 65% versus 29% number. Highest awareness of any of the options, lowest explanation cost once the first batch of residents has been through it and the three common questions are already answered in a template.

**Third: bundled services.** Highest demand of all at 71%, but it needs an approved partner and a pricing sheet first, so it goes last even though it is wanted most. The order is set by rollout cost, not by demand size.

**And one thing not to do:** do not launch all three in the same week. The failure mode is not picking the wrong option, it is picking all of them at once, watching the staff fall behind the per-resident step, and having all three quietly stop being offered within a month. That is how a property ends up back in the 29%.
