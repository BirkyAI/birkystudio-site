I know some agents who call at high level use Fello to stay in front of prospects between follow ups. Two questions, any feedback on it? An agent posted that in r/RealEstateTechnology this week, and it is worth reading twice, because it is not the question it looks like.

Nobody in that post is asking how to make more calls. They are asking how to stay in front of the people they already called. And they are asking what that costs, per seat, forever.

That is the most traditional book of business in this industry. A phone, a list of expired and canceled listings, and a living made out of calling them. If an agent whose whole business is the dialer is shopping for follow-up software, the follow-up is the hard part. The call is the easy part.

## An expired listing is not a lead

A lead raises a hand. An expired listing does nothing. It sat on the market, it came off, and the owner is somewhere between annoyed and resigned. Nobody is going to send you an email about it.

So nothing in your CRM chases them. Your CRM chases the people who filled in a form. The expired list is not a funnel with stages in it, it is a spreadsheet with dial outcomes in it, and the only follow-up system attached to that spreadsheet is your memory.

Your memory says something like: I called her about nine days ago, and I think she said the price was the problem.

Was it nine days, or three weeks? Was it the price, or the kitchen? Nobody knows, because nothing was written down, and there is no version of this where you remember forty of those correctly.

## The week as it is actually built

*The week below is a composite. The shape of it is not.*

```
Mon   8:00 - 9:00    expireds, new calls
      9:30 - 11:00   showings
Tue   8:00 - 9:00    expireds, new calls
      11:00 - 1:00   listing appointment
Wed   8:00 - 9:00    expireds, new calls
      2:00 - 4:00    CMA for the Mill Street listing
Thu   8:00 - 9:00    expireds, new calls
      4:00 - 5:00    paperwork
Fri   8:00 - 9:00    expireds, new calls
      3:00 - 5:00    open house prep
Sat   10:00 - 1:00   open house
Sun   (nothing)
```

Count the blocks. There is a block every weekday morning for the first call. There is no block anywhere in that week for the second call.

That is the whole problem, and it is not a discipline problem. Nobody looks at that week and decides to skip the callbacks. The callback has no hour, so it happens in the cracks, and the cracks fill up with whatever is loudest.

Here is the arithmetic. One hour of dialing is roughly forty calls if you are quick, and forty a week over three weeks is a hundred and twenty names, which is about the size of a decent expired list. So it takes you three weeks to work the list once.

The morning you finally reach name one hundred and twenty, name one was called three weeks ago, and the sentence you said to yourself when you hung up ("call her back in two weeks") expired a week ago without telling anybody.

## One field, and the twenty seconds after the call

The industry has a phrase for this. An agent put it perfectly in the same sub: "Less smart, more impossible to forget to log."

That is the design brief. Not a smarter CRM. One field you cannot avoid, filled in during the twenty seconds between hanging up and dialing the next number. The call outcome, and one line of what was said.

Everything I have written about this before comes down to the same twenty seconds, in [The Note You Didn't Write Is Costing Deals](/blog/phone-note-chaos-transaction-calls/) and again yesterday in [Why Your Follow-Ups Get Ignored](/blog/why-your-follow-ups-get-ignored/): the message that earns a reply is specific, and it can only be specific if the specific thing was written down while it was still in the room.

- It will not dial the list for you. Cold calls placed by a computer are restricted by platform rules and by law, so we do not do them, and the calls stay yours. Anyone selling you a machine that rings strangers on your behalf is selling you a liability, which is roughly what happened in [eleven thousand dials for one lead](/blog/outsourced-isa-prospecting-zero-deals/).
- It will not make the second call sound like the first if nothing was written down after the first. An empty record produces a generic call, and generic calls are why the callback gets skipped in the first place.
- It will not promise a listing, a reply or a meeting. Nobody can honestly promise those, and the agent in that post asking for "similar results" should be suspicious of anyone who answers with a number.
- I will not invent you a competitor's monthly price, and I will not invent a dial-to-listing ratio either. Both would look convincing and neither would be true for your list.
- It needs the record to be somewhere you will actually put it. We configure around your habits rather than asking you to acquire new ones, but no system writes down what nobody says.
