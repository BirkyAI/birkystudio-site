I have never met an agent who intended to leave the phone unanswered. Nobody plans that. What happens is smaller and more ordinary: you plan the time off, and the phone keeps its own schedule.

An agent on Reddit put the real shape of it in one line.

> *"First responder wins, but agents can't be at their desk 24/7"*

That line usually gets quoted as an argument for being faster. I read it as a description of a gap. There are hours in every week when you cannot pick up, and the run from the 22nd of December to the first working week of January puts more of those hours back to back than any other stretch of the year.

## Two weeks, printed as a calendar

Every business has a version of this. Mine looks like the stretch below.

*This calendar is a composite, built from the holiday weeks I have watched agents take over the years. No client details in it, and nothing in it is invented.*

```

Wed 23 Dec   two showings, in the car     arrived: one call at 11:05, missed
Thu 24 Dec   family                       nothing arrived
Fri 25 Dec   family                       nothing arrived
Sat 26 Dec   out of town                  arrived: one text at 8:15pm, answered next morning
Sun 27 Dec   out of town                  nothing arrived
Mon 28 Dec   out of town                  arrived: one website inquiry, found on the 31st

Sun 3 Jan    showings                     nothing arrived
Mon 4 Jan    office, planning the week    nothing arrived
```

## What the calendar actually says

Eight people tried to reach that agent across the fourteen days. Four of them got an answer the same day. Two got one eventually: the text that came in on the Saturday night was answered twelve hours later, and the website inquiry from the 28th waited three days. And two calls were never returned at all.

Two missed calls in two weeks does not look like a business problem. It looks like a holiday. That is exactly why it is worth counting, because the arithmetic is brutal in the right direction: the agent was unreachable on most of those days and still only lost two calls. Imagine the same calendar in a normal week, repeated fifty times a year.

The part I would sit with is not the two calls. It is that nobody can tell you what they were. A missed call writes nothing down. No name, no note, no listing, no number saved. A busy week leaves a paper trail and an empty week leaves a gap, and the gap is where the January pipeline quietly goes.

## The three fixes that do not survive January

I have watched all three of these get set up in early December with real conviction.

**Forward everything to your own cell.** This works until the first Christmas Eve call about a leaking water heater from a client who looked you up six years ago. Then it stops working, and you turn the forwarding off.

**Hire an answering service.** A monthly bill, an operator who has never seen your listings, and a message that lands in your inbox in someone else's words. It is not nothing, and for some businesses it is the right call. It is also a person, which means a schedule, a turnover problem and a script that keeps getting older.

**Promise yourself you will check the inbox every evening.** This is the one I see most, and it dies on the second evening.

The honest pattern underneath all three: whatever covers the phone has to ask nothing of you on the day you turn it on. Anything that needs remembering will be remembered until mid-January.

## What I will not promise

- I will not promise it answers every call. If your carrier sends a call to voicemail before the forward fires, there was nothing for it to answer, and no assistant on earth fixes that.
- It takes a message and routes urgent calls. It does not price a home, negotiate, or make decisions on your behalf.
- It will not fix December. The two calls in that calendar are gone. What it changes is the next two weeks, not the last two.
- I will not quote you a call-answer rate for your line. Anyone who hands you a percentage before measuring your own phone is making the number up.
- It will not make you available. You will still be at the table on the 25th. What changes is what the phone is doing while you are there.
