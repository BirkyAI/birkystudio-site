An agent on Reddit put the whole job in a single line.

> *"One property = 5 separate jobs"*

Photograph it. Write it. Reshape it for every place it goes. Publish it.

And then the one that never makes it onto a to-do list, the fifth job: keeping the versions from drifting apart from each other.

I have watched this happen to careful, organized agents, and it is almost never a discipline problem. It is a counting problem. Nobody counts the fifth job, so nobody schedules it, and if it happens at all it happens at 9pm on a Sunday.

## A listing does not go live once

When a house comes to market, the same handful of facts leaves your desk in four directions at the same moment: same price, same beds, same open house, four different homes for that information.

If you have been selling for a while you already know the four. What is worth looking at is what they say a week later.

*The listing below is a composite, built from the kind of drift I see over and over. No client details, but the pattern is real.*

```
MLS entry, published Friday 9:20am
Price: $389,000
Beds / baths: 3 / 2
Square footage: 1,810
Photo order: kitchen first, then living room, terrace, bedrooms, garden
Open house: Saturday, 10:00 to 1:00
```

```
Flyer, printed Thursday evening, 40 copies
Price: $385,000
Beds / baths: 3 / 2
Photo order: exterior first, then kitchen, then living room
Open house: Saturday, 10:00 to 1:00
Square footage: 1,860
```

```
Portal syndication, live Friday morning
Price: $385,000
Photo order: kitchen first, then terrace
Open house: not shown
```

```
Email to your own list, sent Sunday 8:10pm
Price: $389,000
Open house: Saturday, 10:00 to 1:00
```

Four versions of one house. Now watch a week happen to them.

The seller calls at 8:40 on Friday morning and wants $389,000 instead. The MLS is updated, because you were at your desk. The portal keeps the number it was handed on Thursday night, because nothing told it differently. And forty flyers are already in a bag in your trunk, with one of them on the counter at the shop on the corner. Nobody lied to anybody, and three of the four versions now say something different.

The open house moves to Sunday, because the owner's daughter has a thing. You tell the MLS. You tell your list. The flyer still says Saturday, because the flyer was printed before anyone knew.

The square footage on the flyer came from a 2019 appraisal. The MLS says 1,810. Both numbers are plausible. A buyer who reads both now has a question about you instead of about the house.

None of that is a mistake in the ordinary sense. It is what happens when four copies of the same facts live in four systems that have never met.

## Why nobody catches it

The MLS does not know your flyer exists. The portal takes what it is handed and holds on to it. Your email list is a separate list in a separate tool with its own rules. There is no half hour in your week where the four versions get held up against each other, because there is no single place where all four live.

A different agent described the same thing from the other end of the desk.

> *"I am the integration between two products"*

Between your own four channels, that is exactly the job title. You are the sync. And a person cannot be a sync, because a sync runs constantly and a person is standing in a kitchen with buyers at 11am on a Saturday.

## Three of the four are rented

Here is the part I would tape to the wall.

The MLS is somebody else's database. The portal syndicates your listing and keeps the visitor. Your email list is the closest thing to owned in the group, and it still lives inside a tool that can change its rules on a Tuesday.

That leaves one channel that is genuinely yours: your own website. It is the only place where the listing, the photos, the writing and the inquiry all belong to you, and it is the only one of the four that keeps earning after the house sells. ## What I will not promise

- I will not promise the four versions stop moving. A seller can change their mind on a Friday morning, and nothing in any system prevents that.
- It cannot reconcile a version it cannot see. If a flyer gets printed from a spreadsheet we never touched, that flyer is invisible to us.
- You still approve what goes out. The agent drafts, sorts and chases. You are the licensed human on the record.
- It will not fix a listing with bad photos or a bad photo order. That costs you clicks before it costs you anything else, and it is a photography problem, not a software problem.
- I will not quote you a click rate or an inquiry rate. I have met people who do, and they are making the number up.
