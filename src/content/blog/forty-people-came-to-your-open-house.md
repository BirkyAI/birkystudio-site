It is 4:15 on a Sunday afternoon. The open house ran from one to four, forty people walked through the front door, and the sign-in sheet is now on the passenger seat of your car.

You lock up, you text the seller the number she actually wants to know, and you drive home.

Then Monday happens, and the sheet is still in the car.

An agent on Reddit described this pattern in one line.

> *"Biggest drop-off isn't lead capture, it's the gap between 'someone showed interest' and 'someone is actually booked in'"*

Most people quote that as an argument for buying more leads. I read it as a description of one afternoon. Nobody has a lead capture problem at an open house. Forty people signed in. The problem starts about ninety minutes later, when the most valuable list you own goes into a glovebox.

## The sheet, printed

Here is one of those sheets typed up honestly. Mine is a composite, built from years of Sunday afternoons in the same shape, no client details in it, and nothing in it invented.

```
OPEN HOUSE SIGN IN, Sunday 1:00 to 4:00
14 Ridgeline Court      sheets: 1      lines: 40

 #    Name             Phone         How they heard     Follow-up sent
 1    Dana R.          555-0114      sign in front      text, same night
 2    Priya M.         555-0187      sign in front      none
 3    The Kearneys     555-0142      neighbor           none
 4    Sam O.           555-0163      website            none
 5    Lynn and Bo T.   555-0129      sign in front      none
 6    Adriana V.       555-0175      agent referral     none
 7    Marcus H.        555-0102      drove past         none
 8    Jess and Cal     555-0198      sign in front      none
 9    Mr. Aldridge     555-0151      neighbor           none
10    Tobi A.          555-0136      Instagram          none
11    Ruth P.          555-0119      sign in front      none
12    Camille D.       555-0168      sign in front      none
13-40 twenty-eight more lines, every last column blank
```

Twelve lines printed above, forty on the sheet, and one follow-up in the whole stack.

Two things are worth reading twice.

- **The fourth column decides whether the afternoon was worth anything.** On this sheet it holds one word thirty-nine times: none. Not because the agent did not care. Because at 4:15 on a Sunday, nobody owns that column.
- **The third column is the only marketing research you collected all week, and it is the least reliable line on the page.** Six of the twelve printed above wrote "sign in front", which is the polite way of saying they were driving past. It is the field you will quote to the seller at the kitchen table on Wednesday, and it is mostly noise.

## The column that does not exist

Before lunch, one couple stayed in the kitchen long after everyone else had moved outside, and they said better things than anything on that page.

```
What line 5 said out loud, and where it lives now
  budget             "up to 480, maybe a little more if the roof is new"
  timing             before the school year, so July
  next step          "can we look again Thursday, with our daughter?"
  what they liked    the garden, the light in the kitchen, the quiet street
  what they didn't   the stairs
nothing above is written on the sheet
```

Five facts, four columns, and not one of the four holds the part that makes a follow-up land.

That is the whole problem on one page. The sheet was designed to collect names. The value was in what was said, and what was said had no field. A name and a phone number are enough to send a message. They are not enough to send a message worth reading, which is the difference between a follow-up that gets answered and one that gets ignored. I went through that in [Why Your Follow-Ups Get Ignored](https://birkystudio.com/blog/why-your-follow-ups-get-ignored/), and this sheet is where the generic version comes from.

## Why it always ends in the glovebox

Three jobs wore one jacket that afternoon.

- **Host.** Greet, answer the doorbell, walk people through, keep an eye on the shoes and the kitchen counter, lock up.
- **Note taker.** Write down what each person said while you still remember which person said it.
- **Follow-up.** Get back to all of them, on a cadence, without being asked, starting the next morning.

At 1:40pm on a Sunday the first job eats all of you. The second gets the four lines the sheet asks for. The third belongs to a version of you who does not exist at 8am on Monday with two showings and an inspection report on your desk.

I want to be fair to the sheet, because this is not a discipline problem. Ask anyone who runs open houses and they will tell you the same thing: the list does not die because you stopped caring. It dies because nothing in the afternoon has a slot for the third job, and the list is the only thing at an open house that is both free and warm. A bought list costs money per name and arrives cold. This one costs nothing per name and arrives with the person standing in the house. That difference is worth more than the sheet gets paid for, which is one reason [The Prospect List Nobody Hands You](https://birkystudio.com/blog/the-prospect-list-nobody-hands-you/) is short on names every agent thinks they are missing.
