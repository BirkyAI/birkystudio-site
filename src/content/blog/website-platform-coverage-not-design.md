There is a thread up right now from a brokerage team in British Columbia shopping for a website platform. They have already run two of them, they have a third on the shortlist, and they are asking other agents whether switching was actually worth it.

> "We're looking for something with automatic GVR/FVREB MLS integration, modern designs, good SEO, lead capture, and an easy-to-manage backend... And if you've switched platforms recently, was it worth it?"

Read that requirement list again, because the answer is sitting inside it.

Four of the five things they wrote down, modern design, SEO, lead capture and a backend a non-technical admin can run, are table stakes in 2026. Every platform on any honest shortlist carries all four, which is exactly why the shortlist never gets shorter. The first item on their list is the only one that can rule a platform out, and it is the one item no vendor page answers clearly: does this platform carry our board's feed?

## The scorecard with a blank line on it

When a brokerage writes the comparison down honestly, it looks like this.

| Line on the comparison | Platform one | Platform two | Platform three |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Modern design | yes | yes | yes |
| SEO basics | yes | yes | yes |
| Lead capture and forms | yes | yes | yes |
| A backend an admin can run | yes | yes | yes |
| Carries our board's feed | ? | ? | ? |

Four lines, three platforms, twelve ticks, and not one of them is a reason to pick anyone. That is why the decision stalls for months and why agents end up asking strangers in a forum whether the move paid off. The bottom row is the only line that can disqualify a platform, and it is blank on every comparison because the answer has nothing to do with the platform. It has to do with your board, your province or state, and whether that board licenses its data to that vendor this year.

A platform can be genuinely excellent and still be the wrong answer for you, because it does not carry your feed. That is not a design problem and no amount of good SEO fixes it.

## A site without the feed is a brochure

Strip the listings out and what is left is three photographs, a bio and a contact form. The buyer who lands there has already been on a portal, has already seen the homes, and goes back to the one that shows them. Your site becomes the place they check your name, not the place they search.

And the feed alone is only half the job, which is the part that surprises people. A feed shows homes to anyone who browses. Alerts only go out when somebody turns an inquiry into a saved search, and that step falls to a person.

A different agent, in a different thread, put the same leak more bluntly:

> "I have lead inquiries that come in all day. I rely on our agents to hook them up for MLS alerts via our IDX -- but unfortunately barely anyone does it."

So you can win the platform question, carry the feed, and still lose the lead, because the connecting step is manual and has no owner. I wrote about that leak in [Inbound Leads Die Without MLS Alerts](https://birkystudio.com/blog/inbound-leads-die-without-mls-alerts/), and it is the reason I would rather fix the intake before the platform.

## What a migration actually costs

The question in that thread, whether switching is worth it, has an answer, and it is not a price tag. This is what moves when a brokerage changes platform:

- Every page gets rebuilt in a new editor, by whoever runs the site, on top of their normal week.
- Every URL that changes shape needs a redirect, or the rankings you spent years earning point at nothing.
- The local search history, the review widget, the forms and the place the leads land all get reconnected by hand.
- Whoever administers the site learns a new backend, and that person is usually not technical.
- Whatever feed or integration you had gets rebuilt, or quietly disappears until someone notices.

I am not going to put a dollar figure on that, because it depends on how many pages you have and how much of the last three years you want to keep. What I will say is that it is measured in weeks of somebody's patience, and it is paid before the new platform earns anything.

## What this will not do

- It will not carry a feed your board has not licensed to the platform or the vendor you choose. If your MLS requires a specific vendor's integration, that is a real constraint, and any agency telling you otherwise is guessing about your board.
- It will not win you rankings by itself. Content and links do that, and anyone promising a position is selling you a number they made up.
- It will not chase a channel you have not connected. Nothing works on the inbox nobody set up.
- It will not pretend to be you, and it will not invent a conversion rate. Anyone who gives you a single percentage for every brokerage in every market has never looked at yours.
