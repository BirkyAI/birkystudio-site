There is a line in the tech thread over on Reddit that I have read about thirty times now:

> "First responder wins, but agents can't be at their desk 24/7"

Every agent agrees with the first half of that sentence. Almost nobody likes what the second half implies, so the conversation usually jumps straight to a demo, and the demo answers the wrong question.

The question that actually decides it arrives later, usually by email, usually at ten at night: *if something else answers my phone, does my number change?*

No. And because that one question decides more sales than any feature list, here is the long answer.

## The twenty seconds nobody sees

A missed call feels like one instant to you. To the person on the other end it is a slow sequence, and they judge the whole business inside it.

```
11:02:14  the phone rings, and you are mid-showing
11:02:17  it keeps ringing, the buyer asks you about the roof again
11:02:21  the voicemail greeting starts
11:02:29  the beep
11:02:31  "hi, um, I was calling about the house on Oak Street, I saw it online"
11:02:36  they hang up and dial the next name on their list
```

I have listened to a lot of voicemails with clients, and some of them are four seconds long, because the caller works out in the first sentence that nobody is home and there is no point leaving a message.

They are not leaving because you were rude. They never spoke to you. They are leaving because one of the other numbers on their list answered.

## What actually happens to your number

There are three ways this attaches to a business, and only one of them is usually right.

**Forward on no answer.** Your phone rings first, exactly as it does now, and you pick up whenever you can. When you do not, the line hands the call to the [Voice Receptionist](https://birkystudio.com/products/voice-receptionist/), which answers, takes the details, and can transfer the caller back to your mobile if they ask for you. This is the version I set up most often, because for the caller nothing has changed except that someone picked up.

**Forward everything.** Every call is answered from the first ring, and you get the details afterwards. This suits a business where nobody is standing near the phone during the working day, which is most businesses with fewer than five people in them.

**A second number for after hours and overflow.** Your main line stays exactly as it is, and the number the receptionist answers goes on your website, your Google Business Profile and your ads. This is the lowest-risk version, and the one to pick if you would rather watch it work for a month before your main line is ever touched.

Now the part that matters, and it comes straight off our own product page: it requires a phone number you control and can forward. You keep your number. Nothing is ported, nothing is parked, and no client has to re-save anything.

## Where the call actually goes

```
        caller dials the number on your sign
                     |
        your phone rings, however many times you chose
                     |
        you do not pick up, so the call moves to the receptionist
                     |
        it answers in English or Spanish: "hello, thanks for calling, how can I help?"
                     |
        it answers the usual questions, takes the details, books the appointment
                     |
        caller asks for you? the call transfers to your mobile
                     |
        you get the details in writing: who called, what they wanted, what was booked
```

The step people ask about last, and the one they like most, is the transfer. A caller who insists on speaking to you reaches you, and you never have to explain why you missed the first call.

## The other half is the note

Every one of those calls ends somewhere, and the somewhere is usually your memory.

A different thread, this time from an agent in the middle of a transaction:

> "How do you document phone updates that affect a real estate transaction?"

That is the same disease as the missed call, one step further along. The lender calls with a rate change, the inspector calls with a finding, the title company moves a date, and thirty seconds after you hang up the only copy of that conversation is in your head. I wrote about where those notes go in [The Note You Didn't Write Is Costing Deals](https://birkystudio.com/blog/phone-note-chaos-transaction-calls/).

The receptionist writes it down without being asked: who called, from what number, what they wanted, what was said, and what happens next. You read it in the car, and you walk into the next appointment prepared, which is the part clients actually notice.

## What changes for your callers: nothing

Same number, same dial tone, and the same voice they would have heard on your voicemail. The only difference is that this one answers.

What it will not do matters just as much:

- It will not pretend to be you. It introduces itself as your assistant, and it hands the call over the moment somebody asks for you.
- It will not call anyone. Inbound only. Computer-placed cold calls are restricted by law, and we do not do them.
- It will not decide anything you have not told it. No rates, no legal advice, no promises about a house it has never seen. The calls you flag as "always put this one through" go straight through.
- It is not an emergency line, and it is not an answering service that clocks off at five.
- And I will not quote you a capture rate. Nobody knows how many calls you miss today, including you, because a missed call leaves no record. Anyone who hands you a percentage before measuring your line made that number up.
