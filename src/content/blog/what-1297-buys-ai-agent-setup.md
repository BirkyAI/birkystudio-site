There is a post on r/RealEstateTechnology that I have not been able to get out of my head. An agent paid an outside ISA service for six months. The final tally, in their own words: **11,216 dials, 54 connections, one lead, zero deals.**

Read that again. Eleven thousand dials. One lead.

The comment section, predictably, blamed cold calling. But I think that misses the real lesson. The agent did not just waste money on bad prospecting. They wasted six months *without ever being able to see what they were paying for.* No per-dial visibility, no per-conversation record, no way to audit what the service was actually doing — just a monthly invoice and a "guarantee" that made committing to six months feel safe.

That is the part of the story worth stealing. When you buy a service and you cannot see the work, you are not buying results. You are buying hope.

So when I get on a call with an agent and they ask, *"what does $1,297 actually get me?"* — I love that question. It means they have been burned before and they are not going to be burned again. Fair.

## Where the Money Actually Comes Back

I want to be specific, because "it pays for itself" is a claim anyone can make.

Take the follow-up gap — the thing agents tell me about more than anything else. An agent shows a house. The buyer says "that was nice, let me think." Everyone means well. Nobody has a system. Seven days pass, the buyer is looking at three other properties with three other agents, and the deal quietly dies somewhere between the parking lot and the next appointment.

An AI Agent does not get tired of that moment. It sends the thank-you, the comparable sales, the "still thinking about the kitchen?" check-in on day three. It keeps going at day fourteen when a human has long since moved on.
