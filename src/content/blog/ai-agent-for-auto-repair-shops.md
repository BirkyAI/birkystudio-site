# AI Agent for Auto Repair Shops

Somewhere in your shop right now there is a detail that only exists inside a phone call. A symptom described out loud, a budget mentioned in passing, a warranty date nobody wrote down. By tomorrow it is gone.

A service advisor takes a call at 8:40 in the morning. Cold-start noise, worse on the highway, customer can leave the car Thursday. He writes it on a paper ticket, walks it out to the bay, and gets pulled into the next call. At 3 pm the customer phones back to check on the car, and nobody can find the ticket or remember which vehicle it was for.

Agents describe this exact thing about their own calls. One of them put it plainly in a thread on r/RealEstateTechnology: "How do you document phone updates that affect a real estate transaction?" Different industry, identical shape. The information arrives in a conversation and dies there.

Here is the part that surprises most shop owners. Your problem is almost never the work. You fix the cars. The problem is the record, and the record is where the repeat business lives or dies.

## The note you did not write

Picture a normal Tuesday. Three bays running, the phone ringing between walk-ins, one advisor holding all of it. Every call carries details that matter: the noise, when it happens, how soon they need the car, whether they can leave it, what they can spend.

If none of that lands somewhere that survives the day, it is gone by the afternoon. This is not laziness. It is arithmetic. Ten calls, four bays, one person, and every one of those calls is a small promise you have to keep without a system holding it for you.

## The customer you already fixed

There is a second leak, and it shows up months later. A customer returns fourteen months on, new noise, and the shop has no idea what was done last time, which parts went in, or whether any of it is still under warranty. So the whole relationship starts over at zero.

Buyers and sellers describe the same thing in the real estate threads, where an agent who cannot recall a past conversation ends up spending a longer appointment "to compensate." The customer can feel it. Being remembered is most of what trust is built on, and a paper ticket from last spring is not memory.

## Why it keeps happening

The record lives in two places, and both of them fail. A phone call, which nobody can search. A paper ticket, which does not follow the car and does not survive a busy Tuesday. Neither one is a bad tool. Together they are just a gap with a shop wrapped around it.

## What the agent actually does in a shop

A Birky Studio AI agent takes over the part that keeps falling through:

- It answers the phone, the texts, and the WhatsApp messages around the clock. A lot of people call about a noise after work, when you are already closed.
- It asks the questions you would ask: year, make, model, the symptom, when it shows up, how soon they need the car, whether they can leave it.
- It books the appointment and sends a reminder, so fewer people simply do not show.
- It keeps one running record per vehicle. Every call, every quote, what was approved, what was declined, the warranty date.
- It follows up on quotes that went quiet, on day one, day three, and day seven. Declined work is not always a no. Sometimes it is a "not this month."
- It sends the service reminder when the next interval comes due, so the car comes back to you instead of to whoever reminds them first.

None of that replaces the work you do. It protects the details around it.

## What it will not do

I am not going to oversell this. It will not diagnose a car or turn a wrench. It will not invent customers you never had. It will not pretend to be you, and you should be suspicious of any tool that offers to.

And I will not hand you a percentage. Nobody measured your shop. If a vendor quotes you a number like "40 percent more bookings," ask them which shop they measured and how.
