## 1. We refuse to sell you a fake-flat accuracy number

The 2-3% figure above isn't a lie *on its own* — it's the average across standard tract homes in a metro market. The problem is that a LOT of valuation tools display it as a clean, universal promise, with no confidence interval, no caveat, and no "this is shaky in a low-volume ZIP."

If your neighborhood has 12 sales a year, nobody on earth is pricing your listing within 2.3%. Pretending otherwise isn't service, it's marketing.

So the agents we build never hand you a single number and call it truth. They surface a **confidence range** with every estimate — the number *and* how sure we are about it. Tight range? Trust it, run with it. Wide range? **Escalate to an appraiser**, and the agent tells you to, instead of letting you quote a number that's going to fall apart at the appraisal stage.

That's not more impressive to look at. It's more useful — and it's honest. If you want a screen full of fake precision, we're not your vendor.
