A solo agent on r/RealEstateTechnology put it plainly: *"I've been a solo agent for the last 5 years and haven't paid for any leads. The market has been tough and I'm considering paying for leads again."*

The top reply cut to the real problem: *"You don't really have a lead gen problem, you have a lead capture problem."*

That thread is why I want to be straight about what buying leads actually is. Because most agents are about to spend real money on something they don't own.

## A lead is an event. A prospect is a record.

When you buy a lead from a portal, you are not buying a person. You are buying a moment. Someone typed their name into a form on a listing page, and the portal routes that moment to whoever is paying in that ZIP code.

The moment is rented. It flows to you only while you keep paying, and it stops the instant you stop.

A prospect is different. A prospect is a record you own: a name, an address, a phone, an email, a signal that says this person might sell, and a score that says how likely. That record is yours. It does not expire when your subscription does.

## The part nobody tells you: you share the lead

Here is the part that made me say "are you kidding me" out loud.

On the big portals, the same inquiry goes to up to five agents in the same ZIP at the same time. You are paying top dollar for a lead that three or four other agents also paid for. The consumer is told "a local expert will call," not that five agents are racing to it.

So the fastest caller wins, and the other four paid for a lead they never reached. One agent put it bluntly: *"even though you are paying for the leads, the same lead goes to multiple agents so you better be quick."*

Another found out the hard way: *"Found out three other agents in my office had been paying for the same 'exclusive' leads."*

That is not ownership. That is renting attention you share with strangers.

## What the math actually looks like

Portal leads convert at roughly 0.4 to 1.2 percent. Your own sphere and referrals convert at 15 to 25 percent. That gap is the whole story.

Cost per lead is a vanity number. What matters is cost per closed deal. A shared lead that costs you $165, at a 2 percent close rate, means you spend $6,600 to $10,700 of lead money for every closing that comes out of it.

Agents report the real numbers on Reddit: *"Spent about $8-$9k over their 1 year contract and not 1 deal."* And: *"I didn't get a SINGLE closing from Realtor.com and I paid them $20k!!"*

And the quality problem is not rare. *"Most all leads are bots, scams, kids."* *"around 90% of the seller leads I obtained were either generated by bots or were spam."*

## The follow-up is on you

Here is the part that makes the whole thing worse. The portals hand you the raw inquiry and do nothing else. No CRM, no dialer, no follow-up. One benchmark found the average response time is over six hours, and 48 percent of bought leads never get a second follow-up.

So you are paying a subscription for a shared, unqualified lead, and then you are expected to do all the work to turn it into a conversation, against four other agents racing the same person.

## What you actually own

When you buy a lead, you own nothing. The portal owns the consumer relationship. A returning buyer goes to whoever pays for that ZIP today, not to you. Cancel the subscription and the stream stops, and in many cases so does your access to the leads you already paid for.

A prospect list is the opposite. You get a durable file: names, addresses, phones, emails, and the signals that tell you who is most likely to sell. It stays yours. It compounds. You are not renting a moment, you own the record.
