## The number nobody in that thread added up

On r/RealEstateTechnology a solo agent put the whole dilemma into two sentences:

> "I've been a solo agent for the last 5 years and haven't paid for any leads. The market has been tough and I'm considering paying for leads again."

Then he said the quiet part out loud. When he did pay, years earlier on a team, "the quality of leads weren't the best."

Every reply in that thread argues about quality, and you can see why. Quality is the only thing you cannot inspect at the moment you buy. The price is visible. The delivery speed is visible. Whether the eighteen names are any good is invisible until the invoice clears.

But there is one number nobody in that thread works out, and it is the number that decides the question. A bought lead is priced per lead, so it is priced per month. A monthly price is the easiest number in business to stop counting.

*The statement below is a composite, built from lead invoices I have seen and from the pattern that thread describes. The figures are illustrative. The shape is not.*

## One month, as the vendor bills it

```
Statement, one month
Leads delivered          21
Credited                  3     bad contact details
Billed                   18     at $45 each          $810
Disputed                  1     wrong area           no adjustment
Messages sent by you      0     you were in two closings
```

Two things are wrong on that page, and they are different problems.

The first is the list. Three of twenty-one names were unusable, and the vendor agreed, which is what a credit line is: a number that rings out, an email that bounces straight back, a duplicate of a name you were billed for last month. Then there is the disputed line, the one name in an area you never agreed to buy in. You argued it, and the vendor made no adjustment.

The second is emptier and worse. Zero messages sent. Eighteen billed names landed during the two weeks you were in closings, and not one of them heard from you. That is not a laziness story, it is a timing story, and it is the reason the vendor's number never tells the whole truth. You did not spend $810 on eighteen leads. You spent $810 on eighteen names you never worked, which is the most expensive kind of lead there is.

## What eighteen months of that adds up to

$810 does not feel like much. Monthly bills are designed that way. So add it up the way nobody on the invoice ever will:

```
Month 1                    $810
Month 3                  $2,430     you have passed the $1,997 Lead Generation Pack
Month 6                  $4,860
Month 12                 $9,720
Month 18                $14,580
```

Somewhere in your third month, that bill has already cost more than the entire Lead Generation Pack, which is a verified list, the outreach set up, and a receptionist that catches the replies, bought once.

I could stop there and let the arithmetic do the selling. I am not going to, because those two spends are not the same product, and pretending otherwise is how lead vendors get away with their own arithmetic.

## What $1,997 actually is, and is not

What the pack is: a verified, enriched list of real prospects in one niche and area, scored by opportunity, that you own forever. Outreach set up for you, email first, sent from your own account, with accurate sender information and a clean opt-out in every message. A receptionist that catches and qualifies every reply, day and night. The list size is agreed with you up front, and it is not unlimited.

What it is not: eighteen leads a month. Up to about twenty-five verified prospects are not eighteen bought leads, and I am not going to line them up as though they were.

The comparison I will make is narrower, and it is the only one that matters here. One of those two spends recurs and the other one does not. If the sentence at the top of this post describes you, if the market is tough and you are considering paying for leads again, then you are not making a purchase, you are taking on a bill. There is nothing wrong with taking on a bill. There is a lot wrong with taking one on without working out what it costs by month eighteen.

## The half of the job nobody owns

The vendor's job ends at delivery. There is no line item for working the names, no deadline on the first message, and in a solo practice no role that owns it. It is the same shape as every other step that quietly does not happen, which is why inbound leads die when nobody hooks them into a search ([Inbound Leads Die Without MLS Alerts](https://birkystudio.com/blog/inbound-leads-die-without-mls-alerts/)).

That is the part of the thread the replies skip. Eighteen leads worked properly beat forty leads ignored, and the ignored ones are the ones you already paid for. ## Six things it will not do

- **It will not promise you leads, deals or revenue.** Nobody honestly can, and a vendor who does is selling you a forecast, not a list.
- **It will not stop a lead bill you keep running.** If you buy leads as well, both bills keep going. This replaces a habit, it does not replace a purchase.
- **It will not deliver a fixed number of replies.** What you get is verified, scored contacts, not a leads-per-month promise.
- **It will not chase by cold WhatsApp, cold text or computer-placed calls.** Those need consent you will not have, so the outreach is email, from your own account.
- **It cannot work a name you never open.** The receptionist catches the replies. Somebody still has to decide to send the first message, and that is you.
- **It refuses to quote a conversion rate.** I have never measured how many bought leads close against how many verified prospects close in your market, and the three-of-twenty-one credit line above is an illustration, not a statistic.
