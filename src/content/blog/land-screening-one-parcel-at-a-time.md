Ask land investors how they find their next deal and you rarely get a tool. You get a spreadsheet, three county websites, a call to a planning clerk, and a shrug.

One of them put the wish plainly on Reddit this week:

> *"Rather than identifying individual parcels and researching what can be built on them, I'd like to define specific development criteria and automatically identify all qualifying properties."*

Read that twice, because it is not a data problem. Every parcel he is asking about is public record. It is an order-of-operations problem. Almost every tool in this business researches forward: you pick a parcel, then you ask what can be built on it. He is asking for the reverse. Define the questions first, then find every parcel that answers them.

Forward research does not scale. Backward screening does. The whole gap is in that sentence.

*The criteria, the parcel counts and the timings below are a composite drawn from how land screening actually runs, not one client's file. The wishes are real; the spreadsheet is standing in for a dozen of them.*

## One parcel is already a full afternoon

Agents describe their own version of this every week, and it lands in one line:

> *"Shouldn't need 8 tabs + spreadsheet just to answer one property question"*

On a house, that one question is usually price. On land, it is "can I build what I want here?", and answering it opens a tab for every part of the answer:

```
criterion                  where the answer actually lives
zoning and permitted use   county GIS, plus the zoning code in another tab
deed restrictions          the recorder, in the deed itself
covenants and HOA rules    a private document nobody publishes online
buildable area             a survey, or a lot map plus the setback rules
setbacks and easements     the plat map, plus whatever was recorded later
utility availability       the water and sewer provider, by phone
legal and physical access  the assessor record, plus a look at the road
soil and topography        the soil survey, plus a topo map
subdivision potential      the planning department, on request
```

Nine criteria, and no two of them live in the same place. Six are on a website, two need a phone call, and one may not exist anywhere you can read without asking the seller's association.

For a single parcel that is a solid afternoon and a good day's work. It is what happens at parcel four hundred that matters.

## The cost is paid per parcel

Do the arithmetic that never makes it onto a brochure. If each criterion takes a quarter of an hour of reading, calling and cross-checking, one parcel is about two and a quarter hours. Nine quarters of an hour is 135 minutes.

Now hand the same job to a county with four hundred candidate parcels. Four hundred times 135 minutes is 54,000 minutes, which is 900 hours, or about five months of full-time work for one person, spent asking the same nine questions and discovering, again and again, that the parcel fails at criterion four.

Here is the part that should change how you buy tools. The cost of screening is paid per parcel. It does not shrink as the list grows, the ninth parcel costs exactly what the first one did, and the work never amortizes. You are not buying a cheaper answer. You are buying the same answer four hundred times.

## A filter that answers two of the nine is a sort

Parcel search tools exist, they are genuinely useful, and they are not this. A public parcel search will usually hand you the owner, the acreage and the zoning, and sometimes the assessed value. That is two or three of the nine, and they are the two or three that were already easiest to find.

It knows nothing about the HOA covenant, the recorded easement, whether the sewer actually reaches that lot, or what the soil does after a wet spring. So it returns a list of matches that survive the easy checks and die at criterion four, and you find out on the site visit. It will also, quietly, hand you the same parcel twice under two address formats, which is a known headache of its own ([we wrote about that one here](https://birkystudio.com/blog/duplicate-property-records-cost-you-deals/)).

A filter that answers two of the nine questions is not a filter. It is a sort. The sorting is real work, and it is not the work you needed done.

## What changes when the criteria go in first

Screening backward flips the order. You write the criteria down once, as a list with a pass mark against each one, and that list becomes the input. The machine then does the part a person cannot do four hundred times.

- One criteria sheet, written once: the nine checks above plus any of your own, each with the threshold that passes it
- A screening pass across every candidate parcel in the area, every criterion recorded either as a value or as the word unknown
- One row per parcel, holding a parcel identity that survives the county changing its address format between sources
- A shortlist that carries its own reason: which criteria passed, which came back unknown, and which one killed it
- A scheduled re-run, so a parcel that failed a zoning rule that later changed walks back onto the list on its own
- Every unknown routed to you first, because the phone call you have to make is the one thing we will not fake

The last two matter more than they look. Public records [move slowly and change without telling anyone](https://birkystudio.com/blog/planning-agenda-commercial-leads/), and a screening you ran once is a snapshot, not a watch. The value is in the second run and the third.

- Some of the nine answers are neither free nor online. Covenants, a survey and a soil test have to be requested or bought, and we will name every one of them before you commit to anything
- A screened shortlist is not a feasibility study. It tells you which parcels fit your criteria on paper, not what an engineer finds on the ground
- We cannot promise a number of qualifying parcels, a deal, or a return. We can promise that every parcel gets the same nine checks, in writing
- It does not replace the call to the planning department, and it should not. Some answers come from a person, and we flag those rather than invent them
- If your criteria list is two or three items long, you do not need this. A parcel search and an afternoon will do it, and we will tell you so
