An operator on r/RealEstateTechnology posted a set of numbers that should be taped to every property manager's monitor:

> *"65% of residents want security deposit alternatives, but only 29% of managers actually offer them. 71% value bundled services (insurance, utilities, amenities), yet only 22% of managers provide that option."*

That is pain point #19 in the running thread I keep coming back to, and notice what it is not. It is not a technology problem. The tools to offer deposit alternatives exist. The tools to bundle insurance and utilities exist. Utility setup at move-in, which the same thread flags as the single biggest resident friction point, is a solved problem on paper.

The demand is sitting there, measured to the point. The supply is on the shelf. And the two are not meeting.

## This is a rollout gap, not a tech gap

Here is the shape of it, in one table.

| What residents want | What they actually get | The gap |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Security deposit alternatives | 29% of managers offer them | 36 points |
| Bundled services (insurance, utilities, amenities) | 22% of managers offer them | 49 points |
| Utility setup at move-in | Flagged as the biggest friction point by 30% of managers | Still mostly manual |

Read the middle column again. Nobody in this business decided "we are against deposit alternatives." Nobody sat in a meeting and voted down bundled insurance. The numbers in that middle column are not the result of a decision. They are the result of a **step nobody did**.

I have watched this exact pattern on the agent side of the same forum. One broker wrote that lead inquiries come in all day, and the one thing that would convert them, hooking each lead up to matching MLS alerts, "barely anyone" on their team actually does. Same shape. The demand arrives, the fix is available, and the rollout step falls to a busy human who has nineteen other things at 4:47 PM. So it quietly does not happen. I wrote about that one in [Inbound Leads Die Without MLS Alerts](/blog/inbound-leads-die-without-mls-alerts/), and the mechanics are identical.

## Why the step never gets done

Because it is not one step. It is one step **per resident, forever.**

To roll out deposit alternatives you have to: mention them at move-in, answer the questions that follow, send the enrollment link, follow up when the resident ignores it, confirm the choice, and log the whole thing into the resident's file so the next person who talks to them is not starting blind.

Multiply that by every unit, every turnover, every renewal. The policy is written once. The rollout is manual, repeated, and invisible when it does not happen. So managers who intend to offer the option end up offering it to the residents who happen to ask, and the 65% who wanted it never hear about it.

That is not a lazy team. That is a manual process losing to a busy calendar.
