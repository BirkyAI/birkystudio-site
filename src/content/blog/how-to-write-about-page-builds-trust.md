Most small business owners treat their About page like an afterthought. They throw up a quick paragraph about when they started the company, maybe add a photo, and move on. But here's the thing: your About page is usually one of the top three most visited pages on your website. People click it because they want to decide if they can trust you.

If your About page is boring, generic, or missing entirely, you are leaving money on the table.

## Why People Visit Your About Page

When someone lands on your About page, they already know what you sell. They clicked because they want to know who you are. They're asking themselves a few questions:

- Is this a real business or a scam?
- Do these people know what they're doing?
- Will they understand my specific problem?
- Can I see myself working with them?

Your About page needs to answer all four of those questions. If it only talks about your company's history and mission statement, you're missing the point.

## Start With the Customer, Not Yourself

The biggest mistake business owners make is starting with "We are a company that..." or "Founded in 2018, we pride ourselves on excellent customer service." Nobody cares about that. At least, not yet.

Instead, open with something that shows you understand your customer's world. Here's an example:

**Weak:** "Birky Studio is a web design company based in Guatemala that builds professional websites for small businesses."

**Strong:** "Your customers are searching for you online right now. If they can't find you, or if your website looks like it was built in 2012, they'll hire your competitor instead. We fix that."

The second version speaks directly to the reader's problem. It shows empathy. It makes them think, "These people get it."

## Tell Your Story (But Make It Relevant)

After you've hooked the reader with their problem, now you can talk about yourself. But keep it short and relevant. Your story should answer one question: why should I trust you with my money?

Here's a simple framework:

1. **The problem you noticed** - What gap in the market made you start this business?
2. **Your background** - What experience or skills make you qualified?
3. **Your approach** - How do you do things differently from everyone else?

For example, if you run a restaurant, don't just say "Chef Maria trained in Paris." Instead, try: "Chef Maria spent 8 years in Parisian kitchens, but she always missed the flavors of home. So she came back to Guatemala and created a menu that combines French technique with local ingredients."

That tells a story. It's memorable. It gives the reader a reason to choose you over the place down the street.

## Add Social Proof

Your About page is the perfect place to include a few testimonials, client logos, or stats that prove you deliver results. Don't overdo it, just two or three strong examples.

Here are some ideas:

- A short quote from a happy customer
- The number of projects you've completed
- A recognizable client or partner logo
- An award or certification
- A before-and-after result

Social proof works because people trust other people more than they trust businesses. When a real person says you did great work, that carries more weight than anything you write about yourself.

## Use a Real Photo

This sounds obvious, but you would be surprised how many small business websites use stock photos or have no photos at all. A real photo of you or your team builds instant trust. It tells visitors there are actual humans behind the business.

You don't need a professional headshot (though that helps). A clean, well-lit photo taken with a smartphone works fine. Just make sure:

- The lighting is good (natural light is your friend)
- The background is clean and not distracting
- You look approachable (a genuine smile goes a long way)
- The image is high enough resolution to not look blurry

If you have a team, include a group photo or individual shots. People want to see who they'll be working with.

## Include a Clear Call to Action

Your About page should end with a next step. Don't just let the reader finish and wander off to another page. Tell them what to do next.

Good options:

- "Ready to get started? Contact us today for a free consultation."
- "See our work in action. Check out our portfolio."
- "Have questions? Send us a message and we'll get back to you within 24 hours."

Make the CTA button or link obvious. Use a contrasting color and clear text. Don't make people hunt for a way to reach you.

## Keep It Short and Scannable

Most people don't read web pages word for word. They scan. So break up your About page with:

- Short paragraphs (2 to 3 sentences max)
- Subheadings that tell a story on their own
- Bullet points for lists
- Bold text for key phrases
- White space so the page doesn't feel cramped

A good About page is usually between 300 and 600 words. Long enough to tell your story, short enough that people actually read it.

## The About Page Checklist

Before you publish, make sure your page includes:

- [ ] An opening that speaks to the customer's problem
- [ ] Your story told in a relevant, engaging way
- [ ] At least one piece of social proof
- [ ] A real photo of you or your team
- [ ] A clear call to action at the end
- [ ] Short paragraphs and scannable formatting

If your current About page is missing any of these, now is a great time to update it. It's one of the easiest improvements you can make to your website, and it can have a real impact on how many visitors become customers.
