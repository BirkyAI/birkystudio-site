Real estate agents said it first. In a thread about what actually breaks their business, one of them wrote it plainly:

> "Biggest drop-off isn't lead capture, it's the gap between 'someone showed interest' and 'someone is actually booked in'."

Read that again and swap the nouns. A law firm has the exact same leak, and it costs more per drop.

## For a firm, intake is the business

A real estate agent loses a commission when an inquiry goes cold. A law firm loses the case. There is no pipeline behind the first call: the person who phones you at 8:40 on a Tuesday night, or fills in the form on a Sunday, is deciding in that moment whether to keep looking.

Most firms treat intake as a reception problem. It is not. It is the entire top of the funnel, and it is usually the least supervised part of the business.

## Where the case actually dies

We mapped how a typical small firm handles a new inquiry. The leak is not at the front. It is in the follow-up after the first contact, every time.

| Stage | What usually happens | What it costs |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Call after hours | Rings out to voicemail. Nobody calls back until the next business day, if at all. | The caller dials the next firm on the list within the hour. |
| Web form submitted | Lands in an inbox. Someone reads it between matters, sometimes two days later. | By then the urgency has passed and the person has moved on. |
| Consultation booked | No confirmation, no reminder. The day arrives and the person forgets. | A booked consult that becomes a no-show is a case you already paid attention to and lost anyway. |
| Consultation happened | If they do not retain on the spot, nobody follows up. | Warm, qualified, already-talking-to-you people go quiet and never get a second touch. |

Read the middle column again. Nobody decided any of that. It is what happens when the connecting step falls to a busy human, per inquiry, forever.

## Why the step never gets done

It is not laziness. It is arithmetic. Every new inquiry needs the same small sequence: answer it, ask the qualifying questions, book the consult, send the confirmation, remind them the day before, and then follow up if they go quiet. That is six steps per person, and your people are already inside client matters.

So the steps that do not have a deadline attached are the ones that quietly stop happening. The call that came in at 8:40 PM does not have a deadline. The follow-up on the person who did not retain on the spot does not have a deadline either. That is precisely why they slip.

## What the agent does instead

An AI intake agent sits on your phone line, your website form, and your WhatsApp, and it does the six steps without being asked:

- **Answers every call, day or night.** No voicemail dead end. It greets the caller, takes the basics, and tells them what happens next.
- **Qualifies before you spend time.** Practice area, location, timeline, whether they have already spoken to another firm. You read a summary, not a raw voicemail.
- **Books the consultation** straight into your calendar, with a confirmation and a reminder so the no-show rate drops.
- **Follows up on its own cadence.** Day one, day three, day seven for anyone who went quiet after the first contact, which is the exact leak from the table above.
- **Keeps one running record per person.** Every call, text, and form answer in one place, so whoever picks up the matter is not reconstructing the conversation from scratch.

That last point matters more than it sounds. The same agents who complained about the booking gap also complain that the important context dies in an unstructured phone call. The agent writes it down as it happens.

## What it will not do

I am not going to oversell this. An AI intake agent does not give legal advice, and it should never pretend to be your attorney. It does not run a conflicts check for you, that stays a human step. It cannot conjure cases out of thin air if nobody is finding your firm in the first place. And it will not turn a bad fit into a good client, it just makes sure the good ones do not fall through the gap.

What it does is remove the one recurring step that keeps failing: the follow-up that nobody has time for.
