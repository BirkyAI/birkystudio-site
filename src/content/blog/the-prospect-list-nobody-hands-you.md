## A lead is an event. A prospect is a record.

A lead is an event. Someone filled in a form at 11pm because a photo looked nice. By morning it is a timestamp with a phone number attached, and the only thing it tells you is that a person was briefly curious.

A prospect is a record. A person, the facts that make them a candidate right now, the reason they are on your list at all, and a way to reach them that was still true the last time somebody checked.

Per-lead programs sell you events. In most of them the same enquiry is also offered to whoever else bought the programme that month, so you are paying to race people you have never met to a phone number that is already lukewarm. Nobody is being dishonest about that, it is simply the model: an introduction is rented and then rented again.

Which means the honest question is not "how many leads a month". It is "what do I have on my desk in March".

## Here is the whole file

This is the shape of one record from a verified prospect list. Every line exists to answer a question you would ask before you dial.

```
Name        Marisol A.
Where       Free-standing home, owner on title 4 years
Signal      Value up sharply since purchase, no cash-out refinance on record
Why her     Equity plus no recent refinance: the likeliest seller on that street
Verified    11 October 2026, by hand, against a public record
Reach       Email with opt-out attached
Score       8.1 of 10, and why it scored that way
```

Seven lines. Read what a per-lead program gives you instead: a first name, a phone number, and a timestamp.

- **Where they are.** Whether someone owns or rents, and how long they have held it, is public record, and those two facts decide whether a person is a candidate at all. A rented address on a seller list is not a prospect, it is a mistake with a phone number.
- **The signal.** The thing that moved this person onto the list this month: equity built up, no refinance on record, a neighbour listing, a permit filed. A prospect without a signal is just a name in a spreadsheet.
- **Why her.** One plain line so you know what to say on the first attempt. Not a script, a reason. You should be able to read it out loud and sound like yourself.
- **Verified.** The date somebody checked it, and what they checked it against. A list with no verification date is a guess in a nicer font, and you will discover that on the phone.
- **Reach.** How to contact her, starting with email, carrying a clean opt-out. Which channels are deliberately left out is the next section, and it matters more than the ones included.
- **Score.** A number so you know who to call first on Monday, and the reasoning behind it so you can disagree with the machine. A score you cannot interrogate is not information, it is decoration.
