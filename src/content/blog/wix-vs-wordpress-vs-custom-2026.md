You need a website for your business. You have three options: a drag-and-drop builder like Wix, the open-source powerhouse WordPress, or a custom-built site from a professional studio like Birky Studio. Each has its place. But for a small business in Guatemala in 2026, the answer is not as simple as "just use Wix."

Let us break down what each option actually costs, what you get, and which one makes sense for your business.

## Wix: Fast Start, Low Ceiling

Wix is tempting. You can build a decent-looking site in an afternoon. The drag-and-drop editor is intuitive. Templates look modern out of the box. For a freelancer, a side project, or a temporary landing page, Wix works fine.

**What it costs:**
- Business plan: about $23 per month
- Pay your domain separately: $15 per year
- No developer needed, but no developer available either

**What you do not get:**
- Real SEO control. Wix handles basic meta tags, but you cannot fine-tune schema markup, breadcrumbs, or hreflang tags for bilingual audiences.
- Scalability. If your business grows and you need advanced features like a booking system, a membership area, or an AI agent, Wix locks you into its own limited ecosystem.
- Speed. Wix sites load slower than custom-built sites because you pay for the convenience of their builder framework on every page load.
- Ownership. You do not own your design. You cannot migrate it. If you outgrow Wix, you start from zero.

**The hidden cost:** Wix is cheap until you need something it cannot do. Then you pay a developer to rebuild everything from scratch on a proper platform. That migration cost is often more than a custom site would have been in the first place.

**Verdict:** Good for a one-page portfolio or a wedding invitation. Not for a serious business that plans to grow.

## WordPress: Powerful, But Not for Beginners

WordPress powers 43 percent of the web. It is flexible, open-source, and has a plugin for almost everything. If you are technically inclined and willing to learn, you can build a powerful site.

**What it costs:**
- Hosting: $10 to $30 per month for decent shared hosting
- Domain: $15 per year
- Premium theme: $60 to $200
- Essential plugins (SEO, security, caching): $100 to $300 per year total
- Maintenance: $50 to $150 per month if you hire someone to manage updates and security

**The real cost is your time.**
- Initial setup takes 20 to 40 hours for a basic business site
- Ongoing updates (plugins update constantly) take 2 to 4 hours per month
- Security patches are not optional — outdated WordPress sites are the most hacked platform on the web
- If something breaks (a plugin update conflicts, a theme stops working), you either fix it yourself or pay a developer

**What you do not get:**
- Guaranteed performance. WordPress relies on plugins for speed optimization, caching, and security. Each plugin adds complexity and potential conflicts.
- A unified design vision. WordPress themes give you a framework, but getting every page to look exactly right usually requires custom CSS or a developer.
- Seamless AI integration. You can add a chatbot plugin, but connecting it to a trained AI agent that knows your business inside and out requires custom development anyway.

**Verdict:** Good for content-heavy sites (blogs, news, directories) where you have the time and technical comfort to manage it. But the $50 per month hosting plus $100 per month maintenance adds up to $1,800 per year for a site you manage yourself.

## Custom Website from a Studio: Higher Investment, Highest Return

A custom website from a professional studio like Birky Studio costs more upfront. There is no monthly subscription for the platform. There is no plugin stack to maintain. You own everything.

**What it costs:**

**What you get:**

**The ROI math:** A custom site costs more upfront than a year of Wix. But if that custom site brings you one extra client per month through better SEO and an AI agent that answers calls at night, it pays for itself in the first month. Wix cannot do that. WordPress can, but only with significant custom work that erases the cost advantage.
