The short answer: sure, you can try. The long answer is why I wrote this post — because I've seen a dozen small business owners burn hours and frustration on DIY chatbot builders, only to end up with something that confuses customers and captures zero leads.

## What a DIY Chatbot Actually Is

When most people say "chatbot," they mean one of three things:

1. **A no-code builder like Tidio, ManyChat, or Botpress** — drag-and-drop flows with if-this-then-that logic
2. **A ChatGPT wrapper** — paste your FAQ into a prompt and embed a chat widget
3. **A free WordPress plugin** — usually abandoned, breaks on updates, answers wrong

Here's what these all have in common: **they only respond to what you hard-code.** A visitor asks something you didn't anticipate — "Do you work with restaurants?" or "What's your refund policy?" — and the bot either says "I don't understand" or routes to a generic fallback.

After helping clients clean up failed DIY setups, I can tell you the typical results:

- **Set-up time:** 6 to 20 hours (learning curve + building flows + testing)
- **Lead capture rate:** 5–15% of visitors who interact
- **Maintenance:** ongoing — every FAQ change means updating flows
- **Monthly cost:** $20–$100 for the builder tier that actually does anything useful

Most small businesses give up after two weeks. The chatbot sits dead on their website because no customer trusts a robot that can't answer a simple follow-up question.

…then a $20/month chatbot builder is not your solution. It's a distraction.
