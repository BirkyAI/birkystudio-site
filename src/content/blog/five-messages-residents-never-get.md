There is a thread in the real estate tech subreddit from an operator in this business that reads like a shopping list of things nobody is buying:

> "65% of residents want security deposit alternatives, but only 29% of managers actually offer them. 71% value bundled services (insurance, utilities, amenities), yet only 22% of managers provide that option."

Read that twice and the shape of it gets strange. Every number in the thread is a demand number, and every demand number is larger than the supply number sitting next to it. Residents want the thing. Managers have the thing. The two never meet.

The instinct after reading a thread like that is to go and look at software, and the thread's own replies explain why that is the wrong first move: the tools to offer these options mostly already exist. Nothing needs to be invented. It needs to be rolled out, per resident, on a schedule that nobody owns.

Which brings me to the part that actually gets skipped. Between the demand and the offer there is a message, and in most buildings that message was never sent.

*What follows is a composite, drawn from the same handful of move-ins I have watched go right and go wrong in this segment. The building is not a specific one and the names are not real. The pattern is.*

## The five messages

**1. The one that goes out on day one.**

```
Hi Marcus, welcome to 4B. Your keys are ready at the office until 6 and the
parking fob is in the envelope. To get power and water switched into your name
before the weekend, use this link, it takes about four minutes. Anything broken
when you walk in, reply here and it goes straight onto the maintenance list.
```

Utility setup at move-in is the single biggest friction point in that thread's data, named by 30 percent of managers. It is also the easiest message on this list to write, because the link already exists and the office already has everything it needs to fill in the blanks.

**2. The one that goes out on day three.**

```
Hi Marcus, while I have you: you paid a full deposit up front. There is a
second option most residents never hear about, where a smaller non-refundable
fee replaces part of that deposit. Same unit, same terms, you keep more cash on
day one. Want me to send the details?
```

65 percent of residents want this. 29 percent are offered it. The distance between those two numbers is a message, and nothing else.

**3. The one that goes out on day seven.**

```
Hi Marcus, one more thing and then I will stop crowding your inbox. Your unit
is not covered for renters insurance yet, and we can bundle it with the utility
account you just set up, so it is one payment instead of two. One-page summary
attached if you want it.
```

71 percent value bundled services. 22 percent are given the option. The message costs nothing to send. It only has to be sent.

**4. The one that goes out on day thirty.**

```
Hi Marcus, you are a month in. Quick question: is anything in the unit not
working the way you expected, and is there anything about the building you had
to figure out the hard way? I would rather fix it now than hear about it at
renewal.
```

This one is not an upsell. It is the cheapest renewal insurance there is, because the resident who mentions the noisy fridge on day thirty is not the same resident who lists four grievances in a review on month eleven.

**5. The one that goes out in month nine.**

```
Hi Marcus, your lease runs through June and I know that feels a long way off.
Two things. If you are planning to stay, I can lock your renewal rate now. If
you are thinking about moving, tell me early and I will make the handover as
easy as I can. Either answer is a fine answer.
```

That is five messages. Not one of them is technical. Not one of them needs a product built, a budget approved or a lawyer consulted. Which is exactly why none of them get sent.

## Why the messages do not go out

Ask a manager why and the honest answer is rarely "we forgot". It is three things, and none of them is laziness.

- **No owner.** The person who decides the deposit alternative policy is not the person who talks to the resident on day three, and the leasing agent who talks to the resident cannot change the offer. The step falls into the space between two jobs and stays there.
- **No deadline.** Nothing breaks if the day-seven message is never sent. There is no late fee for a missing check-in, which is the same reason [inbound leads die without anybody hooking them into an alert](https://birkystudio.com/blog/inbound-leads-die-without-mls-alerts/). The work with no consequence attached is the work that quietly does not happen.
- **No memory.** Even a manager who does send all five is sending them out of their own head, per resident, across however many move-ins that month brings. Nobody keeps five dates accurate for forty people in a spreadsheet.

That is the adoption gap hiding inside those numbers from the thread. The tools exist, the demand is documented, and the rollout still does not happen, because a rollout is not a decision. It is five dates per person, repeated forever.

## What it will not do

I would rather set this boundary before someone buys the wrong thing.

- It will not offer a product you have not approved. No deposit alternative, no insurance bundle and no amenity gets mentioned until you have signed off on the wording.
- It will not sign your insurance partner, negotiate a utility rate or set a price. Those are business decisions, and they stay yours.
- It will not promise a renewal, and it will not pretend a resident's mind is made up when it is not.
- It will not send anything you would be uncomfortable seeing quoted back to you, which is a bar I would hold any message-sending tool to.
- It cannot chase a channel nobody connected. If your leasing inquiries land somewhere the agent is not wired into, they stay invisible.
