# AI Agent for Accounting Firms: The Chase Is the Real Work

It is the first week of October, which means the extended filing deadline is a week away and every accounting firm I talk to is living in the same place: the follow-up list.

Here is the thing nobody says out loud. The returns are not the bottleneck. The documents are. And the document chase is the one job in the firm that nobody owns.

The frustration reads exactly like the agents over on r/RealEstateTechnology:

> "I have lead inquiries that come in all day. I rely on our agents to hook them up ... but unfortunately barely anyone does it."

Different industry. Identical shape. Something needs to happen for every single client, there is no deadline stapled to it, and it lands on a busy human one client at a time. So it quietly does not happen. That is the whole problem, and it is why I wrote this post.

## Nobody Decided This Would Be Your Job

Ask an accountant what they do and they say: tax, bookkeeping, advisory, year-end, cleanups. Ask what they actually spent last Tuesday doing and the answer is usually chasing. Chasing statements, chasing signatures, chasing the client who promised everything by Friday and sent one thing on Tuesday.

None of that shows up on an engagement letter, and none of it has an owner. It just accumulates in an inbox and a mental list until someone has time. There is never time.

## The Chase Ledger

This is what a single client relationship actually requires beyond the returns, and what happens to each item right now:

| What you need | Who chases it today | Touches it usually takes | What it costs when it stalls |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Prior-year return and trial balance | Whoever remembers | 2 to 4 emails | The return starts late and review gets compressed |
| Bank and card statements | The client, eventually | 3 to 5 reminders | Month-end close slips into the next month |
| W-2s, 1099s, P60s, receipts | Nobody after the second ask | Unbounded | Extension, or an estimated-payment penalty |
| Signed engagement letter | You, once, then it sits | 2 | Work begins with no protection in place |
| A slot for the review call | Front desk | 2 to 3 | The call slides a week, then two |
| "I will get that to you Friday" | Nobody | Unbounded | The client quietly disappears |

Read the middle column again. Almost every row says nobody. Not because anyone is lazy, but because the chase has no deadline, no queue, and no system. It lives in one person's head, and that person is also running a firm.

## It Is Arithmetic, Not Laziness

Forty active clients. Six items each. That is roughly two hundred and forty small, indefinite follow-ups in a busy month. Each one is five minutes when it goes well: find the thread, write something warm and specific, log it, remember to come back.

Five minutes times two hundred and forty is twenty hours a month. That is half a working week spent on the gap between what clients promised and what arrived, and the work only gets done when the person doing it is not already buried in returns.

The items with a hard deadline (the filing, the payroll run, the client meeting) always win. The items without one always lose. That is not a discipline problem. It is how any human triages.

## The Prospect You Forgot in February

The same shape shows up on the front end of the firm.

Someone calls in February wanting to move their books away from a firm they are unhappy with. They get a good call. They say they will send their last two returns. Then March arrives, nobody calls back, and the person who was ready to pay you becomes a note in a spreadsheet.

The drop-off was never the enquiry. It was the gap between someone showing interest and someone actually being booked in. I wrote a whole post about that gap after watching it kill deals in real estate, and it is the same gap here: see [what a missed follow-up actually costs](/blog/cost-of-missed-follow-up/).

## What We Will Not Pretend

I am not going to tell you an AI agent prepares returns, gives tax advice, or replaces your review. It does not sign anything, it does not make a judgment call on a grey accounting question, and it will never pretend to be you.

It also cannot chase a client who only communicates through a channel it does not touch, or produce a document nobody ever sent. What it does is make sure the ask happens, on time, every time, and that every answer lands in a file you can find later. The judgment stays with you. The chasing does not have to.
