*This is a composite. The wording and the timeline are illustrative. The numbers and the pattern come from threads agents have posted in public and from conversations we have had with agents in the month their contract ended.*

Six months. 11,216 dials. 54 connections. One lead. Zero deals.

That is the entire return, printed on a weekly report that arrived every Monday for half a year. On the report it looked like activity. Reading it back afterwards, it looks like a receipt for nearly nine thousand dollars and one wasted spring.

I have this conversation constantly, so this time I wrote it as an interview. The agent is a composite of the r/RealEstateTechnology threads and the agents who call us once the term is up. The questions are the ones their friends ask them when the friends are still thinking about signing.

## Why did you sign up in the first place?

Because the offer was built so that saying no felt stupid.

There was a guarantee. A set number of dials every month. A dedicated caller, not a call centre pool. A script written for my market. And a six month term, which is the part I now understand properly. Not one line of that contract promised a conversation with a human being who wanted to sell a house. It promised activity, and activity is the easiest thing in the world to manufacture.

An agent on Reddit put his own six month version in a thread: 11,216 dials, 54 connections, a 0.48% connection rate, one lead, no deals. Read the middle two numbers again. That is one connection per two hundred attempts.

## What did the weekly report actually show?

Columns. Lots of them. Dials made, attempts per contact, connections, conversations over thirty seconds, leads transferred. Mine never varied much. It was honest and useless at the same time, because every column measured what the dialer did, and not one of them measured what happened to the person on the other end of the line.

That is the part I want you to sit with. If your only visibility into a service is the service's own activity report, you are reading the vendor's effort, not your outcome.

## What was a conversation worth?

I listened to three of them. Every one went roughly like this.

"Hi, is this Mark? Mark, this is Derek calling on behalf of a local real estate team. Just a quick question, do you own the property on Cedar Ridge or are you renting it?"

"I own it."

"Great. And is there any chance you would be thinking about selling in the next six to twelve months?"

"No, not really."

"Okay, well thank you for your time, have a great day."

Twelve seconds, counted as a connection. Every second of it polite and completely without value, because nobody asked Mark anything he had not been asked before, and nobody wrote down a single thing he said.

## What happened to the one lead?

This is the part that still annoys me.

The report said: interested in selling, spoke to caller. A name and a cell number. No note about what was said, what price range came up, whether there was a timeline, whether the person was an owner or an heir, nothing. I called him twice from my own phone, standing in a parking lot, with nothing to open and nothing to read back to him. The third call went to voicemail and I never heard from him again.

I did not lose that lead because the caller was bad. I lost it because everything he learned died the moment he hung up. There is a reason agents keep asking each other, "How do you document phone updates that affect a real estate transaction?" It is the same reason that question has no good answer yet: nothing was ever built to catch a conversation and keep it. You can read more about that in [the post about phone note chaos](https://birkystudio.com/blog/phone-note-chaos-transaction-calls/).

## When did you stop blaming the caller?

Around month four, when I realised the caller was not the problem.

The calls were reaching people. Slowly, expensively, with a lot of wasted dials in between, but they were reaching them. What was not happening was any kind of record. Nothing connected a Monday phone call to a Thursday follow up. Nothing remembered that Mark had mentioned a rental he also owned. Nothing showed me that the same three streets were being called by three different services because the list had been sold more than once.

[The 11,216 dial thread](https://birkystudio.com/blog/outsourced-isa-prospecting-zero-deals/) made the same point better than I can: the money did not disappear into bad scripts. It disappeared into conversations that were never treated as anything you could keep.

## Would you pay for an outsourced caller again?

Only if I could see and keep every conversation, and only if the contract answered a different question. Not "how many dials will you make" but "what will I still have on day 180 that I did not have on day one".

That question is the whole interview. Everything else is a story about effort.

## What this exit interview actually proves

- **The number you are sold measures the vendor, not you.** Dials and attempts are a measure of your supplier's payroll, not of your pipeline.
- **A conversation is not an asset until it is written down.** The single lead in that report was worth whatever the caller remembered, which was almost nothing, twenty minutes later.
- **More dials is the wrong fix.** You can double the volume and still lose every conversation to the same empty space between "that went well" and "what did they say".
