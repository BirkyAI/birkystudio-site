There is a line from a recent r/RealEstateTechnology thread I keep coming back to: *wire fraud barely comes up, despite losses running into the hundreds of millions annually.*

Read that gap again. Hundreds of millions of dollars a year, and the topic is a footnote in a market full of chatter about lead response and CRM features.

The reason it stays a footnote is not that agents do not care. It is that wire fraud does not look like a security problem from the inside. It looks like a busy Tuesday in closing week.

I wrote about [the mechanics of the scam](/blog/wire-fraud-closing-security-agent/) back in September: the spoofed email, the "updated" wiring instructions, the buyer who follows them because the message looks exactly like every other message in the thread. This post is about the part almost nobody has actually solved. Not detection. Ownership.

## Every Step Of A Closing Has An Owner, Except One

A closing already has a dozen moving parts, and each one has a person attached to it. Title owns escrow. The lender owns the loan. The inspector owns the report. The appraiser owns the valuation. If any of those steps slips, you know whose desk to walk to.

Now ask the room a different question: who owns verifying that the wiring instructions in the inbox are the real ones?

Silence. Everyone assumes someone else handles it. That assumption is the whole attack surface.

| Step | Who is assumed to own it | What actually happens | The gap |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Offer accepted | The agent | Escrow opens, everyone exhales | No wire in motion yet |
| Escrow opened | Title | Wiring details arrive by email | Instructions land in the same inbox as everything else |
| Inspection and appraisal | Inspector, appraiser | Reports emailed to the whole thread | Threads multiply, nobody merges them |
| Final week countdown | Everyone, sort of | Volume spikes, everyone is tired | Attention drops exactly when it matters most |
| "Updated" wire instructions arrive | Nobody, specifically | The email looks correct, so it gets opened | There is no verification step to fail |
| Funds sent | The buyer | Buyer follows the instructions they were given | The loss lands on a person, not a process |

Read that last row slowly. The person holding the risk is the one with the least context to judge it. Buyers wire money maybe twice in their lives. The people who run closings every day are the ones in a position to spot the fake. And the current process quietly says, "buyer, you handle this one."

That is the same shape as the [phone note chaos](/blog/phone-note-chaos-transaction-calls/) agents complain about constantly. The lender calls with a rate change. The inspector texts a finding. Title emails a date change. The buyer WhatsApps at 9pm. Every one is a separate thread, a separate channel, a separate half-record. When the real thread and the fake thread look identical, "gut feeling" is the only defense left, and that is not a defense at all.

## The 5-Minute Closing Rule

The fix is small enough to fit on a sticky note, which is exactly why it is worth committing to before the next deal, not during it.

1. **Agree a code word with title before money moves.** One word, agreed at the start, both sides, in the deal file. Verification becomes a check, not a judgment call.
2. **Any wiring instruction, new or changed, gets confirmed out loud on a number you already have.** Not the number in the email. Not a number someone forwarded. A number from your existing contact record.
3. **Never accept urgency as a reason to skip step two.** "We need it today" is part of the attack, not a justification for it.
4. **One channel per transaction.** Communications land in one place, so a fake thread has nothing to blend into.
5. **Log the confirmation.** "Called title at 2:14pm, confirmed account ending 4471, spoke with Maria." Five seconds of writing that saves two days of arguing.

Five minutes on the front end and the scam collapses, because a fraudster can copy a signature block but cannot answer the title company's real phone line.

The hard part was never knowing the rules. It is enforcing them at 5pm on a Friday when nobody has slept and the deal has to close. That is the exact moment humans reliably lose, and it is the reason "we have a policy" is not the same as having protection.

## Where The AI Agent Actually Helps

A Birky Studio agent is not a security product, and I am not going to dress it up as one. It cannot confirm a wire. Only a person can do that.

What it can do is remove the conditions the scam depends on.

**One running record.** Every call, text, email, and WhatsApp message summarized into a single thread attached to the client's file. A message that does not match the real thread stands out because there is a real thread to compare against.

**Alerts on payment details.** Anything that touches wiring instructions or account numbers gets flagged and pushed to a human with the scripted step attached, instead of sitting in an inbox waiting to be noticed.

**Enforcement instead of memory.** The system triggers the check every time, so it stops depending on a tired agent who remembers rule number two at 10am and forgets it by 4pm.

Honest limit: the agent cannot replace the phone call. It makes sure the phone call happens, gets logged, and lives somewhere you can find it three days later when someone asks, "did we verify that?"
