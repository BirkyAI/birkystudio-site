There's a thread on r/RealEstateTechnology that I keep coming back to, because it's the most expensive sentence a realtor can type. A fellow agent shared the exact numbers from six months of outsourced cold calling:

> *"11,216 dials, 54 connections (0.48%), 1 lead, 0 deals."*

Read that again. Eleven *thousand* phone calls. And at the end of it: one lead, zero closings, and a six-month contract they'd already paid for upfront.

That agent didn't do anything wrong. They did what agents are told to do — hire a dedicated prospector so they can stop cold calling and go back to working deals. The problem isn't that prospecting doesn't work. The problem is *who* they outsourced it to, and how invisible the whole thing was until it was too late.

## The Real Problem: You're Paying for a Black Box

Here's the trap that got that agent. The call center's sales pitch sounded bulletproof:

- "We'll make 2,000 dials a month with proven scripts."
- "We have a 90% fulfillment guarantee."
- "You'll see a steady stream of qualified appointments."

So they signed a six-month contract, because the "guarantee" made it feel safe. And that's exactly where it went sideways — because you can't manage what you can't see.

The dials happened. Nobody can argue with the dials. But a dial is not a connection. A connection is not a conversion. A conversion is not a showing. And nowhere in that chain could the agent actually watch what was happening day to day. They got a dashboard that counted calls and a monthly recap that sounded fine — right up until the quarter ended and the contract money was simply gone.

It's the same complaint we heard over and over when we talked to agents about tech: you never see the ROI until you've already paid, and by then it's too late to steer.

## Why a Dial Isn't a Deal

Let me break down why those numbers collapsed, because it's not the cold callers being lazy. It's the model.

**First, the contact rate.** 54 connections out of 11,216 dials is a 0.48% live-contact rate. That's not an outlier — that's what outsourced dialing looks like when the list is scraped and the timing is random. The calls land at the wrong hour, hit voicemail, and burn a dial with nothing to show.

**Second, there's no follow-up.** This is where the model really falls apart, and it ties to something agents tell us constantly: *"First responder wins — but I can't be at my desk 24/7."* When a lead does show actual interest, the machine fires the dial, gets a maybe, and moves on. Nobody re-engages the warm lead on day 3, day 7, or day 14. The interest that did happen just evaporates because there's no system to catch it.

**Third, you have no transparency.** You're paying for volume, not outcomes. If you can't see — at the lead level, every single day — which dials turned into conversations and which conversations turned into appointments, you're flying blind. And flying blind is how you end up with 11,216 dials and nothing to show.
